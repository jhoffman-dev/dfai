On a narrow screen

An iPad held upright, a phone, or a window dragged narrow: the sidebar becomes a drawer and one page shows at a time.

  • Under 944 pixels wide — the sidebar's 224 plus the 720 a note reads at — DFAI puts the sidebar away. That is an iPad in portrait and any phone; an iPad on its side, and a desktop window at its usual widths, look exactly as before.
  • The sidebar is a drawer. Press the Show sidebar button at the top left of the page (or ⌘\) and it slides over the page. Pick a page and the drawer closes by itself. Esc, a tap beside it, or its own Hide sidebar button close it too. While it is open the keyboard stays inside it, and the page behind is dimmed.
  • One page at a time. There is no Split right on a narrow screen: the button and the menu item go, ⇧⌘\ does nothing, and a ⌘⇧-click on a note opens it in a new tab instead. Tabs work as they always did.
  • A split you already had is kept. If the window was split when it got narrow — or the split was remembered from a wider one — only the pane you were working in shows, and its bar carries the Show sidebar button. The other pane is still there with all its tabs and anything you typed in it (its autosave keeps running), and comes back the moment the window is wide enough again. Nothing closes because the screen got narrower — and nothing offers to: Close pane and ⇧⌘\ wait until both panes are on screen.
  • Nothing scrolls sideways. The page takes the width it has; a wide table scrolls inside its own box, never the whole window.
The right sidebar folds by width as before (see The right sidebar): on an iPad in portrait there is room for it beside a note; on a phone there is not.
