People and mentions

Type @ and a name to link a person. A person is a note of the Person type; the mention is an ordinary link, so Obsidian reads it too.

Mention someone

  • @ and a letter lists people: every note with type: person, archived ones left out. A name that matches exactly comes first, then the people you edited most recently. ↓ ↑ choose; Return or a click picks.
  • Picking writes [[Name]] — or the note's path, like [[People/Name]], when another note shares the name — and draws it as a chip with the person's initial. The file keeps plain [[Name]].
  • @ starts a mention at the start of a line or after a space, (, a non-breaking space or an opening quote. Not straight after a letter or digit, so an email like ann@example.com stays as typed, and not in code or inside a link.
  • Names with dots work: @J.R. Hartley finds J.R. Hartley.

Add a new person

  • For a name of up to three words, the list ends with Create person “…”. Return alone never makes one — when it is the only choice, Return is just a new line. Press ↓ to it, then Return, or click it.
  • The new note goes in People/, made from the Person template, numbered if the name is taken there. While it's being made a faded @Name holds its place, then becomes the link.
  • If you change the note before they're made, the link isn't written, and a message says so: “Ann Lee was added to People, but not linked: the note changed while they were being made.”
  • DFAI won't make a person whose note would change what an existing [[link]] opens, or whose name holds # [ ] | or ^, since a link stops there. People who already have such a name are listed greyed out with the reason, like “A|B” cannot be linked: a link stops at |.

Mentioned in

A person's page shows their meetings, what you're waiting on them for, and their open tasks and decisions — see Linked pages. On a person's page the Links panel heads the notes linking to them Mentioned in. Only a note whose type is exactly person counts as a person — for @ and for this heading.

Other spellings of a name

A Person — and a Company — has an Also spelt property (aliases in the file): a nickname or a short form, commas between them in the properties panel. They are for matching a meeting's attendees to the person; DFAI never uses them to correct text. To correct a mishearing of the name, list it under Misheard as (variants) on the same note — or add the name on the Terms page, which puts it there.

A vault whose Person or Company type was made before this has no such property yet: add one called aliases on Edit type as a Multi-select, or give the note an aliases list by hand. DFAI reads aliases either way.

Merge two people into one

The same person can end up as two notes — mara@larkspur.example.com and mara.quill@larkspur.example.com made two, or Tobias Fenn.md sits at the top of the vault and in People/. On the one to get rid of, open the page's … menu (or the right sidebar's Properties) and choose Merge into…, then pick the person to keep.

To merge several at once, tick them in the Person table (or in a People view with Select on) and choose Merge 2 people into… on the bar above it — or ⌘K Merge selected people… — then pick the person they all go into.

Before anything is written, DFAI says what the merge does: how many links in how many notes will open the kept person, the aliases and emails they gain, and any value the two disagree on — the kept person keeps their own, and the other is listed so you can copy it by hand. Merge then:

  • points every link to the other person at the kept one — in text, embeds, properties like a meeting's people, your templates and the filters of saved views — keeping its display text, #heading and ^block;
  • adds their emails to Other emails and their name and aliases to Also spelt, so the next meeting with them finds the kept person;
  • joins lists (projects, tags…) and types, and fills properties the kept person had empty;
  • adds their text at the end, under Merged from …;
  • puts their note in the Trash, where you can get it back in Finder.

A toast says how it went, with a link to Activity. There is no Undo: the Trash keeps the other note. Before the preview DFAI saves what you've typed anywhere and catches the index up, so a link you've just written is found. A link in one of DFAI's own files other than a template, view or dashboard (a type, say), or a view that names the person by file in its SQL, stops the merge until you change it by hand; so does typing DFAI couldn't save. If a note changed after the preview, nothing is merged and DFAI asks you to look again. If a write fails part-way, the toast names exactly which notes were changed and which weren't, nobody goes to the Trash, and merging again picks up where it stopped.

Find people who may be one

On the Person type's page, Possible duplicates (also in ⌘K as Possible duplicate people) lists pairs that look like one person: the same name in two folders, the same email, an alias that is the other's name, or emails that match — mara and mara.quill at one company, mara.quill and maraquill, a typo like marra.quill, or a domain a letter off. At public mail providers (Gmail, Outlook, iCloud…) only the same address written with or without dots counts. Each pair shows why, with Merge… (and a button to keep the other one instead) or Not the same, which DFAI remembers in the vault's settings so the pair isn't offered again.

A meeting's attendee also matches a person whose Also spelt holds their email address.
