Google Calendar

DFAI can connect to your Google account so that, once block syncing arrives, your timeblocks show as busy on a calendar of their own. For now you can connect, make that calendar and disconnect.

DFAI only ever asks to see and change calendars it made itself — never your main calendar or anyone else's. You sign in in your own browser, and the sign-in is kept in your Mac's Keychain, never in the vault. Nothing in DFAI, and nothing that talks to it, can read the sign-in back.

Before you start: a client ID

Google needs to know which app is asking. Make one in your own Google Cloud project, once:

  1. Turn on the Google Calendar API for the project.
  2. On the OAuth consent screen, add yourself and the scope …/auth/calendar.app.created.
  3. Under Credentials, create an OAuth client ID of type Desktop app. Copy the client ID (it ends in .apps.googleusercontent.com), and the client secret if Google shows one.

Connect

  1. Settings (⌘,) → Google Calendar. Paste the client ID into OAuth client ID, and the secret, if Google gave you one, into Client secret. Press Connect.
  2. Your browser opens Google's sign-in. Pick your account and press Allow. Once DFAI has kept the sign-in, the tab says DFAI is connected to Google Calendar; close it. If it couldn't, the tab says DFAI was not connected and Settings says why. While DFAI waits it says Finish signing in in your browser… — Cancel stops waiting.
  3. Back in Settings it says Connected. Press Create “DFAI blocks” to make the calendar blocks will go to. If you'd made it before, choose it in Calendar for blocks instead. It then says Blocks go to DFAI blocks. A calendar made when the app was called Atlas, Atlas blocks, counts as its calendar too: choose it in Calendar for blocks, and DFAI makes no second one.

The client ID and the calendar are saved in the vault's settings, so your other Mac uses the same calendar. Each Mac signs in for itself.

Disconnect

Press Disconnect…, then Disconnect. DFAI forgets the sign-in on this Mac and asks Google to cancel it. The calendar and its events stay on your Google account. If Google can't be reached, DFAI still forgets it and tells you to remove DFAI yourself from your Google Account's third-party access.

If another vault on this Mac is connected with the same client ID, DFAI forgets this vault's sign-in but doesn't ask Google to cancel it, because that could disconnect the other vault too. Settings says so; disconnect the other vault as well to end DFAI's access.

The same sign-in makes Google Docs when you export a note — the first time, Google asks once more, for files DFAI makes in your Drive.

To show your blocks as busy on that calendar, see Blocks on Google Calendar.

The Local API and MCP can only see whether Google Calendar is connected, and read the meeting notes and blocks already in the vault. They can't read your calendar or the sign-in.
