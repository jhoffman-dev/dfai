Finding your way

The sidebar lists everything; each page opens in a panel with a bar across the top.

Sidebar, top

Search ⌘K, Today, Inbox, Weekly review, Graph, Tags, Automations, Activity, Templates, Skills and Terms. Counts show how many notes each holds — the Inbox's counts proposals waiting for an answer too; Activity's red badge counts new errors.

Favorites

Star any page (the ☆ in its top bar) to pin it here.

Types · Views · Dashboards

Built from your vault. The + beside a heading makes a new one.

Pages

Your folders and notes. Near the end, Archive opens the Archive; System, last, is the .atlas folder (types, automations, sources, settings). Templates and skills aren't in it — they have their own pages, Templates and Skills.

Arrange the sidebar

  • One scroll for the sections. The vault's name, the links at the top (Search, Today, Inbox and the rest) and the footer (Settings, the sun-or-moon theme button, sync) stay put. Everything from the first section to the last — Favorites, Types, Views, Dashboards and Pages, in your order — scrolls together in the one area between them, and each open section shows all its rows; no section has a scrollbar of its own.
  • Headings stay in sight. As you scroll through a section, its heading (with its grip and +) stays pinned at the top until the next section's heading takes its place.
  • Open or close a section by clicking its heading (Favorites, Types, Views, Dashboards or Pages). A shut section stays exactly where it is in your order; only its rows fold away, so nothing below it jumps past it.
  • Put the sections in any order. Hover over a heading, or tab to it, and a grip appears just to its left (Move Favorites, Move Pages and so on). Drag the grip, or focus it and press ⌥↑ / ⌥↓ to move the section one place. Open or shut, every section moves in the same one order.
  • The order belongs to the vault. It's saved in .atlas/settings.md, so it follows the vault to another Mac, and each vault keeps its own.
If .atlas/settings.md can't be read (say its frontmatter was broken by a hand edit), DFAI shows a notice naming the file and leaves it alone until it is fixed; the sections can't be moved until then. If a move can't be saved, a notice says The sidebar's order could not be saved and why.

The top bar of a page

  • ← → Back and Forward, or ⌘[ ⌘], or your mouse's side buttons. Each tab keeps its own history.
  • Breadcrumb shows where the page lives, and beside the name a quiet count says what it holds — 5 to process, 3 templates. A page's own buttons (New template, Open the Inbox view) sit in the bar too. Only a note, a template or a type keeps a title on the page itself; press it to rename.
  • Saved / Saving… / Unsaved is the save status. Notes save as you type; ⌘S saves at once.
  • Spark button: Claude beside the page (⇧⌘J) — see Claude in DFAI.
  • Right sidebar button, beside the spark: Show right sidebar (⌥⌘\), pressed while it is showing. See The right sidebar below.
  • Split button, just before the …: Split right (⇧⌘\). See Two pages side by side below.
  • … menu: Link to…, Show in graph, Move to…, Export…, Archive (or Unarchive), Delete…, Split right, Close pane, and more for views and dashboards.
  • × Close pane, at the very end of the bar, once the window is split.

In a narrow window the bar gives way a step at a time: first the folder goes and the note's name shortens, then Saved hides, then the Back and Forward arrows (⌘[ ⌘] still work). The × and the … always stay.

Tabs

Every pane has a row of tabs above its page. Each tab is one place — a note, Today, the Inbox, a type's table, the graph — with its own Back and Forward and, for a note, its own undo.

  • A plain click on a note in the sidebar opens it in the tab you're in. If a tab in that pane already shows it, that tab comes to the front instead.
  • ⌘-click opens it in a new tab just after the one you're in, and switches to it. ⌘⇧-click opens it beside you — see Two pages side by side.
  • ⌘T adds an empty tab (so does the + at the end of the row). ⌘W closes the tab you're in — never the window; closing the last tab leaves an empty one. ⌘⇧T brings back the tab you closed last, with its history. A middle-click closes a tab too, and so does the × on the one at the front.
  • ⌃Tab and ⌃⇧Tab walk along the tabs; ⌘1…⌘8 jump to a tab by its place and ⌘9 to the last. The arrow keys walk the row without turning the pages; Enter or Space shows the one under the focus, Delete closes it.
  • Switching tabs keeps your place. A note you leave is written as you go, and its editor waits behind the tab you moved to — with its undo — so ⌘Z on the way back undoes what you typed there and nothing else. Up to four notes per pane wait like this; the one you looked at longest ago is let go first, saved.
  • Everything comes back after a restart: the tabs, the one at the front, which pane you were in, and each tab's Back and Forward. Tabs you closed are not kept across a restart.
  • There is no limit on tabs. They narrow to make room, then the row scrolls.

Two pages side by side

  • Split with the split button in the page's bar, next to the …; its tooltip says Split right (⇧⌘\). The new pane opens on a copy of the tab you're in, with its history. Or press ⇧⌘\, choose Split right from the … menu, or ⌘⇧-click a note in the sidebar to open it on the other side.
  • Two panes at most. Once the window is split, the split button goes.
  • Each pane has a × at the end of its bar that closes that pane with all its tabs (⌘⇧T brings them back one by one, the front one first). The other pane keeps its tabs; close the left one and the right one moves over. The tab at the front of the pane you're in has an ink line along its top; the other pane's has a grey one, so a glance says where the next note will open.
  • ⇧⌘\ closes the pane you're in, so its hint shows on that pane's × only. Close pane in the … menu does the same.
  • Nothing you type is lost. What you typed in a pane as it closes is saved, and if the other pane shows the same note, it picks the change up.
  • Each tab keeps its own Back and Forward, and the split and its tabs are still there after a restart.

The peek and link cards

A quick look at a note without leaving where you are: the note opens over the table, board or page you are in, and closes back to it.

  • Open it by clicking a row's name in a table, a card on a board, or a link in a note's text. The table, the board and the page stay as they were under it.
  • The header says where the row sits — 3 of 186 in Task table — with the arrows to the row before and after, Saved, and three buttons: Open as full page (in place of the tab under it), Open in new tab (⌘ Return) and Open in split (⇧⌘ Return). A note peeked from a link has no rows to step through.
  • Step through the rows with ↑ ↓ while the focus is on the header or the properties strip, or ⌥↑ ⌥↓ from anywhere, the text too. The rows are the ones the table or board showed as you opened the peek, in its order — far down a long table too, past what is on screen. A board goes column by column.
  • Edit it as a page. The title, the properties strip (+ N empty shows the rest) and the text edit and save as they do on the note's own page. If the note is open in a pane as well, that pane picks the change up. What you type is saved as the peek closes, steps on, or opens the note elsewhere.
  • A link inside the peek shows its note in the peek, in place of the one there; a link to a heading — [[#Goals]] — opens the peeked note at it. ⌘K opens search above the peek, and the result you pick shows in the peek too. Its commands act on the note on screen: Archive this note archives the peeked one. A command that goes somewhere — Open Inbox, Open Today, a new view — writes what you typed, closes the peek and goes there. ⇧⌘N captures over the peek without moving the page under it.
  • A note open in a pane with typing not yet saved is saved first as you peek it, so the two never disagree.
  • Close it with Esc — from the text too — ×, or a click outside it. The focus goes back to the row or the link that opened it. Esc closes only what is on top: a suggestion list or search above the peek first, then the peek.
  • While the peek is up, the shortcuts that make or arrange notes — ⌘N, ⇧⌘D, ⌥⌘N, ⇧⌘\, the tab keys, Back and Forward — do nothing, and ⌘W closes no tab under it. ⌘S saves the peeked note.
  • Link cards. Rest the pointer on a link in a note's text, in a pane or in the peek, or on a relation's value — a task's project in a table, say — and after a moment a card shows the note's title, its type and folder (Project · Projects) and its first lines. Moving off, a key, a click or a scroll hides it. A link to a note that does not exist yet shows no card. Sidebar rows and board cards show none.
  • The peek's … menu has Export… for the peeked note — see Exporting notes.

Not yet: peeking a row of a dashboard's table, a query page or a linked page — those still open the note.

The right sidebar

A panel on the right that follows whatever has the focus. Show or hide it with the Right sidebar button in any page's bar or ⌥⌘\; the × at the end of its tab row hides it too. Whether it is open, and which tab, is remembered per vault.

  • Type — on a type's page or one of its views, the type's editor, first in the row. See Edit a type. The sidebar is a little wider (340px) while it shows it.
  • Properties — the open note's properties, with the same editors as the page: change one here and the file is written. Empty ones fold behind N empty properties. While the sidebar shows them, the note's own properties block leaves the body so there is one place to edit them; hide the sidebar, switch its tab, or work in the other pane and it is back.
  • Backlinks — what links to the note, what it links to, and its unlinked mentions, as the foot of the page has them.
  • Outline — the note's headings; click one to scroll to it.
  • Claude — the chat. See Claude in DFAI.
  • A table's row. Click into a row of a type's table or a view's table — any cell — and the sidebar shows that note's properties under Task · selected row, without opening it. Editing them writes the row's file and the table follows; Open and New tab at the foot open the row's note. Click back into the page around the table and the sidebar follows the page again.
  • A narrow window folds the right sidebar first — under about 1020px with the left sidebar open — and the left sidebar never folds on its own. Asking for the right sidebar there (the button, ⌥⌘\ or ⇧⌘J) hides the left one to make room; ⌘\ brings it back. Esc inside the sidebar puts the focus back on the page.

Hide the sidebar with ⌘\. Settings (⌘,) and the theme button sit at the bottom of the sidebar, beside the sync light once sync is set up. The theme button shows a moon in light and a sun in dark: press it to switch (Switch to dark theme). Until you press it, DFAI is light or dark as macOS is; Settings → Appearance → Match system turns that back on, and turning it off keeps the theme showing. The choice is this Mac's own. ⌘J opens Ask; ⇧⌘J opens Claude in the right sidebar's Claude tab.

Undo, and what DFAI tells you

A change you make from a list is said in a small dark box at the bottom right — Finished “Send the Larkspur invoice”., Clarified “Call the bank”., Moved “Call Mara Quill” to waiting. on a board, Changed “Call Mara Quill”. for a cell — with Undo and a ×.

  • Where it happens. Ticking, moving, deferring or choosing a task on Today, clarifying a row in the Inbox, accepting or rejecting a proposal, Make task on a checklist line, and, in a view's table, board, calendar or timeline, editing a cell, ticking a box or dragging a card or a bar. An accepted proposal and a new task also offer Open; a rejected proposal has no Undo.
  • Undo puts the note back byte for byte — your comments, quoting and spacing as you wrote them — not the old values written afresh. It is refused if the note has changed since, by you, another pane, another app or sync, and the box says so in amber: “Send the Larkspur invoice” has changed since — its properties changed — so the change was not undone. It is refused too while the note has unsaved typing in a pane. A change to a note open in a pane is that pane's to undo (⌘Z), so a view offers no Undo for it.
  • It goes by itself after 5 seconds, and its Undo with it. It waits while the pointer is on it or Tab has reached its Undo; it never takes the focus. Up to three show at once, oldest on top. Today, the Inbox and the proposals each keep one: their next change takes the last one's place, and only the last can be undone. An error stays until you press ×. A failure with nothing to undo — a Make task that failed, or the Undo of a proposal or a Make task that was refused — is said on its page instead, and stays there until you dismiss it.
  • Missed one? Warnings (amber) and errors (red) are also lines in Activity, so one that went while you were looking elsewhere can be found later. A change that worked isn't: the file is its record.
  • Loading. A page or note that takes more than about 150 ms to read shows a small turning ring where it will be; anything quicker shows nothing at all, so nothing flashes.
