Terms

Teaching DFAI how names are spelt. A notetaker mishears the names it doesn't know — a product, a colleague, a vendor. A term is the one right spelling and the ways it gets misheard, and DFAI and Claude both use the list.

Add a term

  1. On the Terms page, press New term.
  2. Right spelling: how it should be written, for example Larkspur.
  3. Misheard as: the ways it comes out wrong, commas between them — lark spur, Larks Burr. One that holds a comma of its own goes in double quotes: "Quill, Mara", Mara Quil. You can leave it empty and fill it in later.
  4. Kind: product, person, company, acronym or other — or leave it at —.
  5. Add term. It's listed as soon as DFAI has read it.

A term is a note of the Term type, made in Terms/ and named after its spelling: Terms/Larkspur.md. A spelling a file name can't hold, like S/4 Ledger, is kept as the note's title. If the vault's Term template has a title of its own, the new term's spelling is written over it. Open a term from its name to add notes about it, or to set Refers to: it points at a Company — the company a product or an acronym belongs to — and only at companies (where the vault's Term type has it — the Terms page works without the type's file). A spelling left empty is refused: “A term needs its right spelling.”

A company's or a person's name can be added too. When [[Right spelling]] already opens a Company or Person note — say Larkspur Payroll, misheard as Larkspar Payroll — no term is made: the misheard spellings are added to that note's Misheard as (variants), after any it already lists, and the rest of the note is left exactly as it was. The page says where they went and what they now correct to: “Added to Companies/Larkspur Payroll.md: Larkspar Payroll, now corrected to “Larkspur Payroll”.” (or “… already lists those misheard spellings.”). Their name stays the right spelling, and their Also spelt still only links attendees and never corrects text. Kind isn't used for them: the note is already a person or a company. Type the name exactly as the note spells it, capitals included — corrections match exactly, so LARKSPUR PAYROLL is refused: “Companies/Larkspur Payroll.md is spelt “Larkspur Payroll”. Add the term as “Larkspur Payroll”, or rename the note first.” A note with a title of its own that isn't its file name is refused too: rename the note or change its title so the two agree, then add the term again. Give at least one misheard spelling: the name alone is refused, since the note already spells it. An archived note, or one in a hidden folder, is never added to: the vocabulary doesn't read it. A note the name opens whose properties can't be read safely (a key written twice, a bracket never closed), or a person's or company's whose variants isn't a list, is refused with the reason, and left as it is. A name that opens any other kind of note is refused as before: “[[Larkspur]] already opens Zeta/Larkspur.md, and would open the new term instead. Rename one of them first.”

Change how it's misheard

Each row's Misheard as is a box on the page — a person's or company's row too, which names the note it lives in under the name (Company · Companies/Larkspur Payroll.md) and writes that note. Change it and press Return or click away: only the variants list at the top of the note changes, nothing else in it. A variant holding a comma is shown in double quotes, so editing the others never cuts it in two; type one the same way. Two spellings that differ only in spaces are one; ones that differ in capitals are two, since DFAI matches exactly as written. A spelling with no letter or digit in it (a lone ~) is not used, and a warning says so under the term; so does one that is a word of another note's name (a lone Quill is corrected although Mara Quill is left as it is). Invisible characters pasted along with a spelling — zero-width spaces, word joiners — are dropped; the joiners that shape a Persian or Indic word or join an emoji are kept. A spelling with nothing visible in it counts as empty.

What DFAI knows

  • Every term's right spelling and the ways it's misheard.
  • Every person's and company's name, as a right spelling that is left as written, and the ways their note says it's misheard (Misheard as), which are corrected to it. The page lists the people and companies that have any, beside the terms. Their Also spelt spellings are not used to correct anything. A note is a person if any of its types is person, as @ counts it; one that is both a person and a company counts as whichever its type names first.
  • Spaces don't matter when DFAI matches, capitals do: Lark  Spur and Lark Spur are one spelling, lark spur another. A longer name is tried before a shorter one inside it, so Larkspur Payroll wins over Larkspur.
  • The top of the page counts them: “2 terms · 9 misheard spellings DFAI puts right — exactly as written, capitals included…”.
  • Templates, notes in hidden folders and archived notes don't count. To retire a term, archive it.

Conflicts

If two notes claim one spelling for two different right spellings — lark spur as a variant of both Larkspur and Larkspur Payroll — DFAI can't know which you meant, so it leaves that spelling alone. The page shows a Conflicts box above the list with each such spelling and the notes that claim it (click one to open it), and under the term: “In conflict, so not used: “lark spur””. Take the spelling off one of them and the conflict goes.

Built in

Term and Company are built-in types: Delete is turned off for both — “Term is built in — the Terms page lists its notes, and DFAI spells names by them — so it cannot be deleted.” Their properties are yours to change.

Where it's used. When a meeting arrives, its misheard names are corrected by this list — every change recorded on the meeting, and undoable from its page.
