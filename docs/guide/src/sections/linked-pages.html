Linked pages

Open a person, a company, a project, an area or a meeting and the page shows what hangs off it — between its properties and its text. Nothing is written into the note: the sections come from its type, so every page you already have has them.

What each page shows

PageSections
PersonMeetings they were in · Waiting for — tasks at Waiting whose Waiting on is them · Open tasks linked with them · Decisions · Linked notes — everything else linked with them
CompanyPeople linked with it — by their Company, or by the company's own Employees or CEO · Meetings held with it · Waiting for — what you wait on from those people · Open tasks linked with it or about those people · Projects · Decisions · Linked notes
Project, AreaWaiting for · Open tasks filed under it · an area's Projects · Meetings · Decisions · Linked notes (an area's own sections are an Area-type note's; a project note with PARA = Area shows a project's for now)
MeetingPeople with their company · Companies · Tasks and Decisions linked with it — the ones that came from it among them
Task, DecisionMeetings linked with it — the one it came from among them
  • Every row opens its note. A heading shows how many it holds; a section with nothing is one quiet line, none.
  • A section opens with its first 8 rows; Show n more brings the rest, Show fewer puts them away. A section holds 200 at most — past that the count reads 200+, and under the rows: These are the first 200; there are more.
  • The sections are live: a meeting that arrives or a task ticked off anywhere shows up as soon as the index has it.
  • Archived notes are left out, as everywhere. A page never lists itself.
  • Nothing linked is dropped. Meetings, and a project's Open tasks, Meetings and Decisions, hold only what names the page in the property made for it — a meeting's People, a task's Project. A meeting that only mentions the person in its text, or a task that links the project somewhere other than Project, is under Linked notes instead.
  • If the sections can't be read at all, the page says so in one line — “What is linked with this page could not be read just now.” — rather than looking as if nothing were linked.

30 days, or all

  • Meetings, Decisions and Linked notes start on the last 30 days; All beside the heading shows everything. An empty one says none in the last 30 days, with All still there.
  • Your choice is remembered for that kind of page on this Mac: turn Meetings to All on one person and every person's Meetings open on All.
  • Meetings and decisions are dated by their date; linked notes by when they last changed. A meeting or decision with no date shows under 30 days too, after the dated ones. Tasks and waiting-fors have no such switch — open work doesn't expire.

Waiting for, and how long

  • Each waiting-for says how long it has waited — waiting 12 days — longest first.
  • The count runs from when the task last changed, the same measure the weekly review uses: DFAI keeps no "waiting since" date, so editing the task starts the count again.
  • A waiting-for is listed once. On a person's page it is under Waiting for, not again under Open tasks.

Open tasks

  • Grouped by status with what to do first on top: Next Action, In Progress, Inbox, Backlog, then Someday and Longterm. Finished tasks are left out. Click a status to fold it.
  • An active project with open tasks and none at Next Action or In Progress says so over the list: “No next action: none of its 3 open tasks is Next Action or In Progress.” It is the weekly review's rule, so a paused or finished project, one with no status, and an area never say it. Nor does a project with more than 200 open tasks, where the list was cut.

Meetings and tasks, both ways

  • A meeting lists the tasks and decisions linked with it, and each of those shows the meeting under Meetings. Any link between the two counts, whichever note holds it: the task's source citing a line of the meeting, a meeting property, the meeting listing the task, or a link in either one's text.
  • So the tasks that came from a meeting are there, and so is a task that merely mentions it — and a task you make by hand joins a meeting the moment either links the other.

What decides the sections

  • The page's type, and what your types declare. A person's meetings are the ones whose People relation holds them; if your Meeting type has no relation to Person, any link from the meeting counts instead.
  • Waiting for needs a Task type with a Waiting status and a Waiting on relation — see Move your tasks to GTD. Without them the page shows open tasks only.
  • Linked notes lists notes of your other types, and what a section's own type links outside its property. A note with no type isn't there — it is under Links at the foot of the page, as before.
  • A company's Meetings are the ones whose Companies holds it. A meeting only its people were in isn't there.
  • A template shows no sections: it isn't a page of its type.
  • To list something else on one page, add a query block; it sits in the note's text beside these.
