Start the app

DFAI runs from its project folder in development mode, which reloads screen changes by itself.

cd ~/Projects/atlas
pnpm tauri:dev

When to restart

  • Screen changes appear live while pnpm tauri:dev is running.
  • Changes to the Rust side (new commands, the index): stop with Ctrl C and run pnpm tauri:dev again. The first rebuild takes a minute or two.
  • New packages: stop, run pnpm install, then pnpm tauri:dev.

The first time, choose your vault folder. DFAI remembers it and reopens it next time. The search index rebuilds itself when it needs to.

Used it when it was called Atlas? The first time DFAI opens on a Mac that ran Atlas, it copies what Atlas kept on that Mac: the vault it had open, the API's token, your secrets, this Mac's sync name and your settings. macOS may ask once for each secret — choose Always Allow. A secret it can't read is asked for again on the next two launches; after that DFAI says which, so you can set it again in Settings → Secrets. Atlas's own copy is left as it was. If Atlas is still open, DFAI asks you to quit it first; if something can't be copied, DFAI says why and doesn't open, and the next launch tries again.

Finding your way

The sidebar lists everything; each page opens in a panel with a bar across the top.

Sidebar, top

Search ⌘K, Today, Inbox, Weekly review, Graph, Tags, Automations, Activity, Templates and Terms. Counts show how many notes each holds — the Inbox's counts proposals waiting for an answer too; Activity's red badge counts new errors.

Favorites

Star any page (the ☆ in its top bar) to pin it here.

Types · Views · Dashboards

Built from your vault. The + beside a heading makes a new one.

Pages

Your folders and notes. Near the end, Archive opens the Archive; System, last, is the .atlas folder (types, automations, sources, settings). Templates aren't in it — they have their own page.

Arrange the sidebar

  • One scroll for the sections. The vault's name, the links at the top (Search, Today, Inbox and the rest) and the footer (Settings, the sun-or-moon theme button, sync) stay put. Everything from the first section to the last — Favorites, Types, Views, Dashboards and Pages, in your order — scrolls together in the one area between them, and each open section shows all its rows; no section has a scrollbar of its own.
  • Headings stay in sight. As you scroll through a section, its heading (with its grip and +) stays pinned at the top until the next section's heading takes its place.
  • Open or close a section by clicking its heading (Favorites, Types, Views, Dashboards or Pages). A shut section stays exactly where it is in your order; only its rows fold away, so nothing below it jumps past it.
  • Put the sections in any order. Hover over a heading, or tab to it, and a grip appears just to its left (Move Favorites, Move Pages and so on). Drag the grip, or focus it and press ⌥↑ / ⌥↓ to move the section one place. Open or shut, every section moves in the same one order.
  • The order belongs to the vault. It's saved in .atlas/settings.md, so it follows the vault to another Mac, and each vault keeps its own.
If .atlas/settings.md can't be read (say its frontmatter was broken by a hand edit), DFAI shows a notice naming the file and leaves it alone until it is fixed; the sections can't be moved until then. If a move can't be saved, a notice says The sidebar's order could not be saved and why.

The top bar of a page

  • ← → Back and Forward, or ⌘[ ⌘], or your mouse's side buttons. Each tab keeps its own history.
  • Breadcrumb shows where the page lives, and beside the name a quiet count says what it holds — 5 to process, 3 templates. A page's own buttons (New template, Open the Inbox view) sit in the bar too. Only a note, a template or a type keeps a title on the page itself; press it to rename.
  • Saved / Saving… / Unsaved is the save status. Notes save as you type; ⌘S saves at once.
  • Spark button: Claude beside the page (⇧⌘J) — see Claude in DFAI.
  • Right sidebar button, beside the spark: Show right sidebar (⌥⌘\), pressed while it is showing. See The right sidebar below.
  • Split button, just before the …: Split right (⇧⌘\). See Two pages side by side below.
  • … menu: Link to…, Show in graph, Move to…, Export…, Archive (or Unarchive), Delete…, Split right, Close pane, and more for views and dashboards.
  • × Close pane, at the very end of the bar, once the window is split.

In a narrow window the bar gives way a step at a time: first the folder goes and the note's name shortens, then Saved hides, then the Back and Forward arrows (⌘[ ⌘] still work). The × and the … always stay.

Tabs

Every pane has a row of tabs above its page. Each tab is one place — a note, Today, the Inbox, a type's table, the graph — with its own Back and Forward and, for a note, its own undo.

  • A plain click on a note in the sidebar opens it in the tab you're in. If a tab in that pane already shows it, that tab comes to the front instead.
  • ⌘-click opens it in a new tab just after the one you're in, and switches to it. ⌘⇧-click opens it beside you — see Two pages side by side.
  • ⌘T adds an empty tab (so does the + at the end of the row). ⌘W closes the tab you're in — never the window; closing the last tab leaves an empty one. ⌘⇧T brings back the tab you closed last, with its history. A middle-click closes a tab too, and so does the × on the one at the front.
  • ⌃Tab and ⌃⇧Tab walk along the tabs; ⌘1…⌘8 jump to a tab by its place and ⌘9 to the last. The arrow keys walk the row without turning the pages; Enter or Space shows the one under the focus, Delete closes it.
  • Switching tabs keeps your place. A note you leave is written as you go, and its editor waits behind the tab you moved to — with its undo — so ⌘Z on the way back undoes what you typed there and nothing else. Up to four notes per pane wait like this; the one you looked at longest ago is let go first, saved.
  • Everything comes back after a restart: the tabs, the one at the front, which pane you were in, and each tab's Back and Forward. Tabs you closed are not kept across a restart.
  • There is no limit on tabs. They narrow to make room, then the row scrolls.

Two pages side by side

  • Split with the split button in the page's bar, next to the …; its tooltip says Split right (⇧⌘\). The new pane opens on a copy of the tab you're in, with its history. Or press ⇧⌘\, choose Split right from the … menu, or ⌘⇧-click a note in the sidebar to open it on the other side.
  • Two panes at most. Once the window is split, the split button goes.
  • Each pane has a × at the end of its bar that closes that pane with all its tabs (⌘⇧T brings them back one by one, the front one first). The other pane keeps its tabs; close the left one and the right one moves over. The tab at the front of the pane you're in has an ink line along its top; the other pane's has a grey one, so a glance says where the next note will open.
  • ⇧⌘\ closes the pane you're in, so its hint shows on that pane's × only. Close pane in the … menu does the same.
  • Nothing you type is lost. What you typed in a pane as it closes is saved, and if the other pane shows the same note, it picks the change up.
  • Each tab keeps its own Back and Forward, and the split and its tabs are still there after a restart.

The peek and link cards

A quick look at a note without leaving where you are: the note opens over the table, board or page you are in, and closes back to it.

  • Open it by clicking a row's name in a table, a card on a board, or a link in a note's text. The table, the board and the page stay as they were under it.
  • The header says where the row sits — 3 of 186 in Task table — with the arrows to the row before and after, Saved, and three buttons: Open as full page (in place of the tab under it), Open in new tab (⌘ Return) and Open in split (⇧⌘ Return). A note peeked from a link has no rows to step through.
  • Step through the rows with ↑ ↓ while the focus is on the header or the properties strip, or ⌥↑ ⌥↓ from anywhere, the text too. The rows are the ones the table or board showed as you opened the peek, in its order — far down a long table too, past what is on screen. A board goes column by column.
  • Edit it as a page. The title, the properties strip (+ N empty shows the rest) and the text edit and save as they do on the note's own page. If the note is open in a pane as well, that pane picks the change up. What you type is saved as the peek closes, steps on, or opens the note elsewhere.
  • A link inside the peek shows its note in the peek, in place of the one there; a link to a heading — [[#Goals]] — opens the peeked note at it. ⌘K opens search above the peek, and the result you pick shows in the peek too. Its commands act on the note on screen: Archive this note archives the peeked one. A command that goes somewhere — Open Inbox, Open Today, a new view — writes what you typed, closes the peek and goes there. ⇧⌘N captures over the peek without moving the page under it.
  • A note open in a pane with typing not yet saved is saved first as you peek it, so the two never disagree.
  • Close it with Esc — from the text too — ×, or a click outside it. The focus goes back to the row or the link that opened it. Esc closes only what is on top: a suggestion list or search above the peek first, then the peek.
  • While the peek is up, the shortcuts that make or arrange notes — ⌘N, ⇧⌘D, ⌥⌘N, ⇧⌘\, the tab keys, Back and Forward — do nothing, and ⌘W closes no tab under it. ⌘S saves the peeked note.
  • Link cards. Rest the pointer on a link in a note's text, in a pane or in the peek, or on a relation's value — a task's project in a table, say — and after a moment a card shows the note's title, its type and folder (Project · Projects) and its first lines. Moving off, a key, a click or a scroll hides it. A link to a note that does not exist yet shows no card. Sidebar rows and board cards show none.
  • The peek's … menu has Export… for the peeked note — see Exporting notes.

Not yet: peeking a row of a dashboard's table, a query page or a linked page — those still open the note.

The right sidebar

A panel on the right that follows whatever has the focus. Show or hide it with the Right sidebar button in any page's bar or ⌥⌘\; the × at the end of its tab row hides it too. Whether it is open, and which tab, is remembered per vault.

  • Type — on a type's page or one of its views, the type's editor, first in the row. See Edit a type. The sidebar is a little wider (340px) while it shows it.
  • Properties — the open note's properties, with the same editors as the page: change one here and the file is written. Empty ones fold behind N empty properties. While the sidebar shows them, the note's own properties block leaves the body so there is one place to edit them; hide the sidebar, switch its tab, or work in the other pane and it is back.
  • Backlinks — what links to the note, what it links to, and its unlinked mentions, as the foot of the page has them.
  • Outline — the note's headings; click one to scroll to it.
  • Claude — the chat. See Claude in DFAI.
  • A table's row. Click into a row of a type's table or a view's table — any cell — and the sidebar shows that note's properties under Task · selected row, without opening it. Editing them writes the row's file and the table follows; Open and New tab at the foot open the row's note. Click back into the page around the table and the sidebar follows the page again.
  • A narrow window folds the right sidebar first — under about 1020px with the left sidebar open — and the left sidebar never folds on its own. Asking for the right sidebar there (the button, ⌥⌘\ or ⇧⌘J) hides the left one to make room; ⌘\ brings it back. Esc inside the sidebar puts the focus back on the page.

Hide the sidebar with ⌘\. Settings (⌘,) and the theme button sit at the bottom of the sidebar, beside the sync light once sync is set up. The theme button shows a moon in light and a sun in dark: press it to switch (Switch to dark theme). Until you press it, DFAI is light or dark as macOS is; Settings → Appearance → Match system turns that back on, and turning it off keeps the theme showing. The choice is this Mac's own. ⌘J opens Ask; ⇧⌘J opens Claude in the right sidebar's Claude tab.

Undo, and what DFAI tells you

A change you make from a list is said in a small dark box at the bottom right — Finished “Send the Larkspur invoice”., Clarified “Call the bank”., Moved “Call Mara Quill” to waiting. on a board, Changed “Call Mara Quill”. for a cell — with Undo and a ×.

  • Where it happens. Ticking, moving or deferring a task on Today, clarifying a row in the Inbox, accepting or rejecting a proposal, Make task on a checklist line, and, in a view's table, board, calendar or timeline, editing a cell, ticking a box or dragging a card or a bar. An accepted proposal and a new task also offer Open; a rejected proposal has no Undo.
  • Undo puts the note back byte for byte — your comments, quoting and spacing as you wrote them — not the old values written afresh. It is refused if the note has changed since, by you, another pane, another app or sync, and the box says so in amber: “Send the Larkspur invoice” has changed since — its properties changed — so the change was not undone. It is refused too while the note has unsaved typing in a pane. A change to a note open in a pane is that pane's to undo (⌘Z), so a view offers no Undo for it.
  • It goes by itself after 5 seconds, and its Undo with it. It waits while the pointer is on it or Tab has reached its Undo; it never takes the focus. Up to three show at once, oldest on top. Today, the Inbox and the proposals each keep one: their next change takes the last one's place, and only the last can be undone. An error stays until you press ×. A failure with nothing to undo — a Make task that failed, or the Undo of a proposal or a Make task that was refused — is said on its page instead, and stays there until you dismiss it.
  • Missed one? Warnings (amber) and errors (red) are also lines in Activity, so one that went while you were looking elsewhere can be found later. A change that worked isn't: the file is its record.
  • Loading. A page or note that takes more than about 150 ms to read shows a small turning ring where it will be; anything quicker shows nothing at all, so nothing flashes.

Notes

A note is a markdown file. Its name is the title you type at the top of the page.

Make one

  • ⌘N makes a note in the folder you're looking at, or at the top level. Made while you're looking at an archived note, it goes at the top level, not in the Archive.
  • Right-click a folder in Pages → New note here.
  • On a type's page, New <type> (New person, New book) — on the toolbar or at the foot of the table — makes a note of that type from its template, in the type's home folder.
  • ⇧⌘N (or ⌥⌘N) opens quick capture: type a line and it becomes a task in the Inbox, without leaving the page — see Quick capture.

Write

Type markdown as you go (# for headings, - for lists, [] for a checkbox) or type / for the block menu: headings, lists, task list, quote, code block, callout, table, divider, Link to note, Image and Query (a live query).

A link to a note can sit on its own line as a card with the note's picture and summary — see Bookmarks.

# with a space after it is a heading; # straight followed by a word is a tag, like #idea — see Tags. @ and a name links a person — see People and mentions.

Properties

Properties sit under the title as rows: a status pill, dates like Tue, Sep 22, 2026, numbers, links to other notes.

  • + Add a property asks for a name and a kind (text, number, date, checkbox, select, multi-select, URL, relation, thumbnail). On a typed note choose Add to <Type> to add it to every note of that type, or Only this note.
  • Relations show the linked notes as chips; click one to open it, × to remove it. A relation that allows many keeps an Add… button.
  • A relation suggests notes as you type — you don't have to remember what a note is called or how it's written. Click the field (— no company — until one is chosen) and type a few letters: it lists only real notes of the type it points at — never a template, nothing in .atlas, nothing archived. A name that starts with what you typed comes first, then one with a word that does, then one with those letters in order (lkpr finds Larkspur Payroll, from three letters). Case and accents don't matter: zoe finds Zoë. A note's aliases count too, and the list says which alias matched. Among equal matches, the notes you link most lately and most often come first. Each row shows the note's type in its colour and its folder.
  • ↓ ↑ move, Return picks, Tab picks the highlighted note (never Create, so a typo then Tab makes nothing), Esc takes back what you typed and closes the list. If the notes can't be read, the list says so. Typing in Japanese, Chinese or Korean works: the Return that ends a word picks nothing.
  • Create <type> ‘…’ is offered when no note in the vault is called what you typed — linked already or not, of any type (Create company ‘Larkspur Payroll’). DFAI makes a new note of that type from its template — the template itself is left untouched — and links it. It goes in its type's home folder — a company in Companies/ — numbered if the name is taken.
  • In a text property, type [[ and the same list offers every note; pick one and [[Its name]] goes in where you typed.
  • A name a link couldn't reach — one holding #, ^, [ or ] — is refused, with the reason: “Acme #1” cannot be linked: a link stops at #. So is a name with nothing left once the characters a file name can't hold (/, :, | and the like) are turned to spaces: Give the new company a name. Nothing is made either way.
  • Dates save when you leave the field or press Return. Use Clear to remove one.
Title and file name. Editing the title at the top renames the file, unless the note has a title property, in which case it edits that. Clearing a title that came from the property removes it.

Pictures in notes

Put a picture anywhere in a note. DFAI copies it into your vault and writes an ordinary markdown image, so Obsidian shows it too.

Paste

A screenshot or a copied image file: ⌘V in the note.

Drop

Drag image files from Finder onto the note. They land where you drop them.

Block menu

Type / → Image and pick a file. It goes where you typed.

Page menu

… in the top bar → Insert image…

  • While a picture is saving, a placeholder holds its place. If it can't be saved, the reason shows there and nothing is added to the note.
  • Click a picture to select it, then type its alt text (a short description) in the Describe the image field under it. Return or Esc goes back to writing.
  • PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP and SVG work, and HEIC when the Mac can draw it. Each can be up to 20 MB. Two pictures with the same name are numbered, never overwritten.
  • Deleting a picture from the note removes it from the note only. The file stays in the vault.
  • Moving a note: Update N links fixes its pictures' paths too.

Where they go: Settings (⌘,) → Images → Images go in: the Attachments folder (attachments/, the default) or Beside the note, in the note's own folder.

Not yet: resizing a picture, and showing Obsidian's ![[picture.png]] embeds as pictures. Those are kept exactly as written, so Obsidian still shows them.

Folders, moving and deleting

Right-click (or hover and press …) on anything in Pages.

  • New folder, New note here, Rename, Move to…, Archive, Delete… — on a folder, Archive N notes; see Archive.
  • Drag a note or folder onto a folder to move it. From the keyboard: select a row, Space to pick it up, arrows, Space to drop.
  • After a rename or move, DFAI says how many links still use the old name and offers Update N links. Nothing is rewritten until you click it.
  • Delete sends things to the macOS Trash, so you can drag them back. The dialog says how many notes and other files go with a folder, and warns about unsaved edits.
  • Views and dashboards are deleted from their own … menu. They stay in their sections and can't be moved.
  • The Archive has rules of its own. Nothing can be moved into it — “Archive a note from its menu, so it can go back where it was.” — and nothing in it can be moved out except by Unarchive. Nothing can be moved or renamed to become the top-level Archive folder: “Only archiving puts notes in the Archive — choose another name.” And the Archive folder can't be deleted in one go: “Delete notes from the Archive instead.”

Exporting notes

Take a note — or a meeting and its transcript — out of DFAI as Markdown, a PDF or a Google Doc. Exporting only reads; it never changes the note.

Start one

  • A note's … menu → Export…, or the same in the peek's … menu.
  • ⌘K → Export…, for the note on screen — or the peeked one.
  • Several at once: in a view or table, press Select, tick the notes, then Export N notes… on the bar — see Act on several rows.

Pick a format

FormatWhat you get
Markdown, as isThe file exactly as it is, byte for byte. Its pictures are copied to a <name> attachments folder beside it.
Markdown, standaloneReads in any tool. A [[link]] becomes its plain words — or, when the notes it names are exported with it, a relative link to their files. Embedded notes and blocks are written in as quotes, under From <note>. Pictures are copied; callouts stay; Obsidian block ids like ^t0001 are dropped. Tick Keep the properties as frontmatter to keep them at the top.
PDFThe note as it reads in DFAI: title, a summary of its properties, the text, tables, checklists and pictures, in the light theme on US Letter pages. A table row or a line is never cut across two pages. A meeting's transcript keeps each speaker's turn with its time.
Google DocA real Google Doc in your Drive, named as the note, in a folder called DFAI exports.

Choose where

  1. Press Export…. For a file, the Mac's own save dialog asks where it goes.
  2. For several notes, pick a folder: DFAI makes a new dated folder in it, Exported notes 2026-10-21, with the notes side by side and their pictures shared in attachments/.
  3. Cancel the save dialog and nothing is written; the export dialog stays open to try again.

Afterwards a notice says where it went — for one Google Doc, with Open in Google Docs. Activity records it under Exports.

Google Docs

DFAI uses the Google Calendar sign-in from Settings → Google Calendar. The first time, your browser asks once to allow one more permission: making and changing only the files DFAI creates (drive.file) — never anything else in your Drive. The sign-in stays in your Mac's Keychain. If Google isn't connected yet, DFAI says to connect in Settings → Google Calendar first.

When it can't

  • The reason is said: the folder can't be written to, Google isn't connected or wasn't allowed, you're offline, or it's too large — a PDF page over 64 MB, or a Doc over 45 MB with its pictures.
  • Pictures that can't be found, or are too big, are left out, and the notice counts them.
  • A PDF or a Google Doc fetches nothing from the web: a picture on the web becomes its words, linked to its address, and is counted as left out too.
  • An export never goes inside the vault, and never into a folder that's already there: if Kickoff attachments exists beside the file, the pictures go to Kickoff attachments 2. If an export fails part-way, what it made is taken away again; nothing that was there before is touched.
  • If one of several Google Docs fails, the ones already made are listed in Activity, and the notice opens the first. Google being busy for a moment is tried again a few times first.

Claude and scripts can read a note's Markdown through the Local API and MCP; PDFs and Google Docs are made only in the app.

Archive

Put a note away without deleting it. It leaves your lists, searches and graph, and can be found and brought back at any time.

Archive a note

Page menu

… in the note's top bar → Archive.

Pages

Right-click a note → Archive. On a folder, Archive N notes archives every note in it; the folder and any other files stay put.

Search

⌘K → Archive this note, for the note you're on. Open Archive is there too.

Several at once

In a table view, Select, tick the rows, then Archive N notes — see Views.

  • A project, an area or a resource is archived where it stands. Every way of archiving above — the page menu, Pages, ⌘K, a view and the weekly review — sets a Project note's PARA to Archive and keeps what it was in para_before_archive; Unarchive gives that back — an area comes back an area — and takes the record off. With nothing readable kept, it comes back a project. The file doesn't move and nothing else in it changes. Setting PARA by hand archives it too.
  • A note of the old Area or Resource type is archived the same way: it becomes type: project with para: archive and para_before_archive: area (or resource), so it comes back as what it was. In a vault with no Project type it is moved instead.
  • A note whose para is something of your own — Life admin, a list — is never written over: it is archived by moving it, as below. Everything below is how every other note is archived.
  • It moves to Archive/ at the path it had — Projects/Plan.md becomes Archive/Projects/Plan.md, numbered if that is taken — and gets archived: <today> and archivedFrom: <where it was> in its properties. Nothing else in the file changes.
  • If the note already had its own archived or archivedFrom property, it is kept aside and given back, exactly as written, when the note comes out.
  • Links: archiving one note offers Update N links, as a move does. Archiving several updates the links to them by itself.
  • A note with unsaved typing is saved first, then archived.
  • On a schedule, or as notes arrive: an automation can archive the notes a query finds — the ready-made one archives done tasks after 30 days; another could file each new standup as it appears. It leaves a note with unsaved typing where it is rather than save it, and its last run can be undone.
  • A note more than 32 folders deep can't be archived: “It is too many folders deep: in the Archive, DFAI would no longer read it.” If some notes in a batch can't go, a message names each one and why.

Out of the way

  • In Pages the whole Archive is one Archive row; click it to open the Archive page rather than a long folder.
  • Archived notes are left out of views and type counts, search, the graph, [[ suggestions and note pickers, the Tags page's counts, and the Links panel's links in and unlinked mentions.
  • A project note with PARA = Archive is left out of the Project type's views and query blocks, note pickers, a board's project groups, the sidebar's Project count, the weekly review, the projects a meeting is linked to, and what Inbox notes can be filed under. Search and the graph still find it, and the Archive page lists it.
  • To reach them anyway: Include archived in search (⌘K) and on a view's toolbar, and the Archived switch on the graph. On a view it turns itself off when you go to another view; search starts without it each time.
  • Renaming a tag still changes it in archived notes, so they read right when they come back.

The Archive page

Open it from the Archive row in Pages, or ⌘K → Open Archive. It lists archived notes — in the Archive folder, and project notes with PARA = Archive where they stand — most recently archived first, with where each Came from and the day it was Archived. A project archived where it stands came from where it is, and shows no day.

  • Search the Archive… matches notes whose title or path holds every word you type, in any capitals — überblick finds Überblick, however the Mac stored its accent. It shows up to 500 at a time; with more, it says More are archived; search to narrow them.
  • Click a name to open the note. Unarchive on its row puts it back.
  • Tick several and press Unarchive N notes. Any that couldn't go back stay ticked, with the reason in a message.

Unarchive

  • From the Archive page, the note's own … menu (Unarchive), or ⌘K → Unarchive this note. Several at once: on the Archive page, or in a table view with Include archived and Select on.
  • It goes back to where the Archive holds it, minus Archive/: Archive/Projects/Plan.md goes to Projects/Plan.md, numbered if something has taken that place since. The archived and archivedFrom properties come off.
  • A project note comes back in one step however it was archived: out of Archive/ if it's there, and its PARA set back to what para_before_archive kept.
  • Editing archivedFrom doesn't change where it goes: that record only spells the same place as the note had it.

PARA and the Inbox

DFAI follows PARA: everything is filed under a Project or an Area, reference is kept as Resources, and finished things go to the Archive. All four are one Project type, told apart by its PARA property. Whatever you capture lands in the Inbox first, to be filed when you process it.

PARA = Project

Something with an end — DFAI 1.0, Move house. Status, the area it's part of, a due date, a summary.

PARA = Area

A responsibility with no end date — Garden, Health, a team. Projects and notes are filed under it just as under a project.

PARA = Resource

Reference worth keeping — an article, a manual, a how-to — kept as a project note of its own.

PARA = Archive

Done or no longer needed. Out of the way where it stands, never deleted, and back the moment PARA is set back.

Set where a project stands

  • A new project shows PARA = Project. Make it from the type's New or a picker's New project…. A project note with no PARA at all, or one DFAI can't read, counts as a project.
  • Change PARA in the note's properties to Area or Resource, and it moves to the Areas or Resources group in every picker. Its type stays Project, its file stays where it is, and links to it keep working.
  • PARA = Archive puts it away — out of the type's views, pickers and the sidebar's Project count, and out of the weekly review. Set it back to Project (or Area, or Resource) and it's back. Archive does the same from anywhere, keeping what PARA was; Unarchive gives that back. See Archive.
  • Area on a project links the area it's part of; its picker lists only areas. It's optional — a project saves without one.

Every note can be filed

  • A Project property on a task or a meeting links a project or an area. Its picker suggests both, each marked Projects or Areas, and offers Create project ‘…’ and Create area ‘…’ for a name no note has — Create area makes a project note with PARA = Area. A resource or an archived one is never offered, and nor is a person, a company or any other note.
  • A note with no type can be filed too: processing it gives it project all the same.
  • Linking the wrong kind of note — a person in a task's Project, typed by hand — shows “Project links to a project or area, not a person” under the property. A link to a note not written yet, or to one with no type, is left alone.
  • Any relation can point at several types this way: in the type's file write target: [project, area]. Edit type shows the first under Points at and the others under And at; switching Allow many keeps them, while choosing another type in Points at points it at that one alone.
  • project, area and resource in a relation's target are PARA values: they point at project notes whose PARA says that. target: [project, area, resource] lists all three groups; target: area, as Project's own Area has, lists only areas.

The types are set up for you

  • PARA is one type, Project. A vault without one isn't given it on its own: it hasn't taken PARA up, so it's offered instead (below). DFAI no longer writes Area or Resource types.
  • A Project type written before PARA was a property is offered it: “Project gains Area, linking area notes. Project gains PARA.” — each only if your type has no property of that name. A property you added to Project stays exactly as you wrote it.
  • DFAI's own types gain what DFAI ships on them. Opening a vault adds the keys DFAI ships to its built-in Meeting, Proposal, Decision, Daily and Block types when a file written earlier lacks them — a Meeting from before Tasks and Decisions, say, which would otherwise refuse to link an action item. Each type changed is a line in Activity, and a record is kept in .atlas/migrations/type-properties.md. A key you already have is never touched — the new keys are lines added after your own, and every line you wrote stays exactly as it was — and one you remove later stays removed. A type DFAI can't add to that way — its properties written as a list, or on one line as {…}, or two files that both say they are the Meeting type (a sync's conflict copy) — is left alone, with a warning in Activity saying why; merge the two files and the next open adds the keys. Project and Task keys are never added on their own: they stay an offer (below).
  • Anything more is offered, not done. On the Inbox page a box lists it — “Adds the Project type.” for a vault without one, “Task gains Project, linking project or area notes.” for a Task type with no project yet, “Meeting's Project will link project or area notes.” for one that links projects only. Add to the types makes those changes against the type files as they are at that moment — an edit you made while the box waited is kept — and keeps everything else in each file byte for byte; Not now puts the box away until the vault is opened again.
  • The Inbox's own types are offered the same way. A vault without them is offered “Adds the Proposal, Decision and Daily types.” — proposals are notes of the first two, and today's note of Daily. Nothing is written until you press Add to the types. (A Meeting type missing what the meeting import writes isn't offered it: DFAI adds those keys itself, above.) A Person or Company type without a Misheard as is offered one — “Person gains Misheard as.” — so the properties panel shows a name's misheard spellings by name; corrections use them either way.
  • Project is built in: it can't be deleted, and its properties are yours to change. Area and Resource types you already have aren't built in any more — delete them once their notes have moved.

Move Area and Resource notes onto the Project type

A vault from before PARA was a property has notes of the Area and Resource types. The Inbox page offers to move them: “Projects, areas and resources are one Project type now, with a PARA property. 12 notes would move onto it.”

  1. Press Preview the move. It lists every note that would move and what it becomes — Garden: Area → Project, PARA Area — what the Project type gains, if anything, and every note it leaves as it is, with why.
  2. Press Move 12 notes, or Cancel. Not now puts the offer away until the vault is opened again.
  • Each Area note becomes type: project with para: area, each Resource note para: resource. A project, area or resource whose file is already in Archive/ gets para: archive, and para_before_archive keeps what it was — area, resource or project — so Unarchive gives it back. It stays where it is.
  • Only type, para and, for an archived note, para_before_archive are written. No file moves, every other property and every line of text stays byte for byte, and a property you added is never removed.
  • A note that already has a para of its own — not Project, Area, Resource or Archive, like Life admin or a map — is left as it is, type and all, and the preview says so: Garden: its PARA “Life admin” is not Project, Area, Resource or Archive, so it is left as it is.
  • What the preview shows is what is written. A note open with unsaved typing, or changed since the preview read it, is left as it was — It changed since the preview read it. — and the run says so in Activity, one line for the move and one for each note left. Run it again later and it moves what's left; with nothing left the offer isn't shown. A note already archived by its PARA is never offered again.
  • Your Area and Resource type files stay until you delete them.

Capture goes to the Inbox

  • Quick capture ⇧⌘N — or ⌃⌥N from any app — makes its task in the Inbox folder at the top of the vault — made the first time it's needed — whatever note you're on. Once your tasks follow GTD, it starts with the status Inbox, whatever the Task template says.
  • Meetings arrive in their month, Meetings/2026/10/, and wait in the Inbox list from there. Anything you drop into Inbox yourself waits there too, of any type or none.

Process the Inbox

The Inbox is one list: every note in the Inbox folder, every imported meeting not yet filed under a project or an area (nor archived) wherever it lives, and every task whose status is still Inbox wherever it lives — one imported into Tasks/, say, or made by accepting a proposal. A task outside the folder with no status at all waits here too.

  1. Click Inbox at the top of the sidebar, or find it in search (⌘K). Its count is how many are waiting, and how many proposals wait for an answer; the weekly review counts the same. (The row shows once something is waiting, or when the vault has a view called Inbox.)
  2. The page lists everything waiting, newest first, with its kind and the folder it came in — Meeting · Meetings. Its heading says how many wait in all — 734 to process. It lists the newest 500 at a time: past that, the foot of the list says Showing the newest 500 of 734. and Show more lists the next 500, so every one can be reached. The page scrolls as a whole, so however many proposals are above it, each row keeps its controls whole. A meeting the import hasn't got to yet says Waiting to be imported; one it couldn't import says why, in red — Not imported: title is required. Click a name to open it.
  3. Clarify a task with one key. With a row focused — click it, or it takes the focus once the row above is done — the keys show on the row: N Next, W Waiting, S Someday, D Done (the task is archived), each written at once as one save you can undo. The row leaves the list as you press the key and the focus is on the next row straight away, while the save is written behind it, so five rows take five keys without waiting on the disk; a save that is refused brings its row back, saying why, and the rows you did after it are still saved. If you have already picked something on the row (a project, a date) and not saved it, a status key writes those picks together with the status — nothing you picked is left behind. T opens the Defer date and P the project picker (P shows only when there is a project or an area to file under). In the project picker a key opened, type a few letters and press Enter (or click a suggestion): the project is written at once, with anything else the row holds. Typing alone writes nothing. A date a key opened is held until you press Enter in it, since a date changes with every digit. Esc closes the suggestions; a second Esc drops what you picked and puts the focus back on the row. Longterm has no key; the status picker still offers it. The keys are live only while the row itself has the focus — inside a picker, a date, a dialog or search they are letters — never with ⌘, ⌃ or ⌥ held, never repeating while a key is held down, and not while a write is on its way. Clicking a key does the same.
  4. Or on its pickers. Pick its Status (the eight GTD statuses), a Project or area, and a Defer or Due day if it has one, then press Save — or Return in a date. Everything you picked is written in one save.
  5. File any other note — a meeting, a plain note — by typing a few letters in File under… and picking a project or an area; it is filed at once. The project, area and Waiting on fields all suggest as you type, the way a relation does in Properties. A project or area no note has yet is offered as Create project ‘…’ or Create area ‘…’: it is made and the note filed under it.
  • Waiting offers who. Pick Waiting and a Waiting on… picker appears on the same row, listing your people. It's optional: press Save with or without someone. Pick a person and the status and the person are written together.
  • A status is enough. A task given any status but Inbox leaves the list, with or without a project — a single action needs none — and stays in the file it is in. (This holds once your tasks follow GTD; in a vault still on statuses of its own, everything in the Inbox folder waits until it is filed.)
  • A project files it — as a link. Filing writes project: "[[DFAI]]"; where the project's own note sits doesn't matter. A meeting in its month folder stays where it is. A note in the Inbox folder moves to its type's home folder for its day — a task to Tasks/2026/10/, a meeting from Inbox/Meetings to its month — and a plain note, or one whose type has no home, to Notes/2026/. It is never moved into the project's folder. Only the note's name goes with it, numbered if the folder has one by that name; nothing else in the file changes, and links to it are updated. A task that already lives outside the Inbox folder is linked to the project and stays where it is.
  • Only a project or an area still in use can be chosen — not one waiting in the Inbox itself, and not an archived one.
  • A refusal shows on the row it was for, in red, and what you picked stays picked. Nothing was written — except when the status was saved and the filing then failed: the row says so, and the task stays where it was with its new status.
  • Only statuses your Task type has. A vault whose tasks are still on statuses of their own is offered only the GTD statuses its Task type declares, and is never given one it doesn't; move the tasks to GTD to get all eight. A person picked while the row said Waiting isn't written if you change the status again before Save.
  • Undo. Once each one is saved, the box at the bottom right says Clarified “Call the bank”. with Undo: the note goes back where it was and says exactly what it said. Only the last one can be undone, and not once the note has been changed since. The box goes by itself after 5 seconds, and the undo with it; it waits while the pointer is on it or Tab has reached its Undo. A checklist line's … is a task now. is said in the same box, with Open and Undo; only its refusal, with nothing to undo, stays on the note's page until you dismiss it.
  • Work down the list. Once a row is done — by a key or by Save — the next row has the focus, so the list is worked top to bottom from the keyboard; Tab walks its name, status, project and dates. A vault whose Task type lacks a status is not offered its key.
  • Make task, on a plain note with no type, makes it a task waiting in the Inbox, to clarify like any other.
  • Proposals aren't filed: they're answered, in the page's Proposals section, and are never in this list.
  • If your vault has a view called Inbox, the page's Open the Inbox view button opens it; it's still there under its own name. The view this repository's vault ships lists backlog tasks, everything under Inbox/ and any meeting that failed import.
  • Claude can do the same through atlas_clarify_inbox_item (a task) and atlas_process_inbox_item (filing).

Tidy the vault, once

A vault from before home folders is offered a tidy on the Inbox page: “Every type has a home folder now. 212 notes could move to theirs, out of the root of the vault and the old Inbox/Meetings.”

Types and properties

A type is a kind of note — Task, Project, Person — with its own properties. Types live as files in .atlas/types.

  1. Press + next to Types in the sidebar, give it a name and an icon.
  2. Open the type's definition with Edit type — see Edit a type below.
  3. Add properties, choose each kind, drag to reorder, tick Required on new notes on the ones every note should have.
  4. For a Relation, choose the type it points at, and tick Allow many for it to hold several notes.
  5. For a Thumbnail, nothing more to choose: each note of the type gets a picture of its own page (see Thumbnails). A type can have only one.

Edit a type

Clicking a type in the sidebar opens its views, not its definition. The type itself is edited in the right sidebar's Type tab, beside its notes. Any of these opens it:

  • The gear at the end of the type's view tabs (Edit Task type, for Task). The view stays where it is, with the editor beside it.
  • Edit type in the bar of the type's own page. It stays pressed while the editor is showing; press it again to put the sidebar away.
  • Right-click the type in the sidebar (or focus it and press ⇧F10) → Edit type. The same menu has Open.
  • Search ⌘K: type edit and pick Edit <type> type, e.g. Edit Task type. These only appear once what you type starts with “edit”, so they never crowd out notes.

Each of these places also has Edit template, for what new notes of the type start as — see Templates.

The editor shows the type's icon and name at the top — click the icon to choose another, click the name to rename it. Below, its Properties, with how many there are. Each row shows the property's name and kind — a relation by the type it points at, Person, many — and its arrow opens it in place:

  • Name and Kind, and for a relation Points at.
  • Allow many — for a select, a relation: several values instead of one. A select that allows many is a multi-select.
  • Required on new notes — written as required: true in the type's file. It never stops you saving a note: an empty required property just says “Date is required” under it until it is filled.
  • Stored as — the key the value is saved under in each note's file.

At the bottom, Template and Default view open what new notes start as and the type's first view, and the foot says Saved · edits apply to every Session note: every change is written to the type's file straight away, and nothing else in that file is touched. The sidebar follows the page: open another type and it edits that one. In a window too narrow for the sidebar (under about 1060px with the left sidebar open), asking for the editor hides the left sidebar to make room.

Options for select properties

Rename, reorder, colour and remove options — for example a project's statuses planned, active, paused, done. (A task's eight GTD statuses are moved to all at once, from the Inbox.) Renaming an option asks “N notes use ‘review’…” and offers to update them. Order sets the order of a board's columns and lanes, and of a table's groups. The Done option decides which status a ticked checkbox sets.

Colours. Every option is a muted pastel pill, so a column of them is easy to scan. A status (Doing, Review, Done…) keeps its own colour and dot. Any other option — a meeting's Interview or 1on1 — gets a colour from its name: the same name is always the same colour, in a table, on a board, in the properties panel and on cards. To choose one yourself, pick it from the option's colour menu: one of the status colours, or gray, brown, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, pink or red. In the type file it is written as colors: { Interview: blue }. Linked notes (a meeting's Larkspur Payroll or Mara Quill) and #tags are coloured from their names the same way.

Built-in types

Person, Task, Project, Artifact, Meeting, Company, Term, Proposal, Decision, Block and Daily are marked Built in: DFAI's own features depend on them. In the type's editor their Delete button is turned off, with the reason beside it — for example “Person is built in — @ mentions find people by it — so it cannot be deleted. Its properties are yours to change.” Their properties stay yours to edit. Their files in .atlas/types can't be deleted or renamed from Pages either, whatever the file is called: “DFAI’s built-in types stay — its features depend on them.”

A type you made has Delete <Type>… under its properties in the type's editor; it asks first, as deleting any note does, and puts the type's file in the Trash.

Projects

DFAI ships a Project type (status: planned, active, paused, done; owner; due; summary). Every task has a project relation — to a project or an area, once your types are set up for PARA — and the Projects view is a board grouped by project.

Templates

A template is what a new note starts as — its properties and its text. Editing one changes the notes made from it next, never the ones already made.

What uses a template

A template's name is what ties it to what it makes:

  • A type's template is called what the type is called — Person for Person, in any capitals. Every new note of the type starts from it: New person on the type's page and at the foot of its table, Create person “…”, and quick add through the API and MCP.
  • Daily is what Today's note starts as — without one, it starts as a note of the Daily type with What happened and What is next headings — Task what a captured task starts as, and Artifact what a saved artifact starts as.
  • Any other template is only offered in the New menu — the + beside the vault's name, under Blank note, Today's note and Claude artifact….

Edit a type's template

  • Edit template at the top of the type's own page, beside Edit type, and the Template row at the foot of the type's editor.
  • Search ⌘K: type edit and pick Edit <type> template, e.g. Edit Person template.

Editing a template

  • It opens like a note — properties, then text — under a band: “Template · Used for: New Person notes. Editing it changes what new notes start as — never the notes already made.” The band has Move to notes… and All templates.
  • There's no favourite star (it would be copied into every new note), and no Move, Archive or graph. Renaming its title renames the template, with the same tie as above.
  • The Dashboard template shows as a note, not a dashboard; what you make from it is the dashboard.

Templates are never notes

  • They aren't in Pages (not even under System), search, the graph, [[ and @ suggestions, relation pickers, links in or out, or unlinked mentions. A template's tags don't count on the Tags page.
  • A link that used to reach a template — [[Person]] once opened the Person template — now shows as missing, or reaches a note of that name if there is one.

A template that was really a note

If a note ended up in the templates folder — say a company saved over the Company template — Move to notes…, on its row or its band, asks “Make “Larkspur Payroll” a note?”: “It moves out of the templates to the top of the vault, exactly as it is. Links to “Larkspur Payroll” reach it again.” It's numbered if a note there has its name, and it says what stops using it, if anything. Then press Move to notes.

Views

A view is a saved way of looking at notes of one type, and a type owns its views. Click a type in the sidebar and its views open as tabs across the top. To look across several types at once, save a query as a view.

A type's views, as tabs

  • Click a type in the sidebar and you see only that type's views, as tabs. It opens on the tab you last had for that type on this Mac, else the first.
  • Opening a view any other way — a link, search, the Views section — lands on the same page, with that tab selected.
  • A type with no views yet shows one default table tab, named for the type (Idea table), with every property as a column. It is only written as a view once you add a view beside it, rename it or duplicate it. It can't be deleted — there's nothing to delete.

Add, rename, copy and delete from the tabs

  • + at the end of the tabs (Add a view) lists the layouts; pick one. The view is named for you, like Task board or Task list (then Task list 2), and opens selected.
  • The … on the selected tab (View options) has:
    • Rename — type the new name in the tab and press Return. It changes the tab's name only; you never need to know the view's file name, and links to it keep working.
    • Duplicate — a full copy, with its filter, sort and grouping, named … copy (Board copy, then Board copy 2).
    • Delete view — asks first, then Move to Trash. You land on the next tab, or the previous one if it was last.
  • Settings are edited in place: Layout, Filter, Sort, Group and Properties in the toolbar, as below.

Reorder the tabs

  • Drag a tab to a new place, or focus it and press Alt+← / Alt+→ to move it one place.
  • The order is saved in the views themselves, so it syncs to your other Macs.
  • Views nobody has reordered keep the old order (by layout, then name), and nothing is rewritten until you change the tabs.

Query views, SQL views and dashboards are unchanged: they belong to no one type, so they keep their own pages and their places under Views and Dashboards.

Make and change views

  • New view: the + on a type's tabs (above), or the + next to Views in the sidebar — pick a name, the type and a layout. That sidebar + also has New query, for a view across types — see Queries.
  • Layout, Filter, Sort, Group and Properties (which fields show) change the view at once. Grouping is below.

  • Include archived lists archived notes too, for now. It isn't saved with the view and turns off when you go to another view.

Filter

  • Filter → pick a property and a condition (is, is not, contains, …), then a value. The view redraws at once; Save view keeps it.
  • The value box offers what the property can hold: a status's or select's options (a task's eight GTD statuses), the vault's tags for tags, true and false for a checkbox, Today, Tomorrow and the like for a date, plus a date picker. For a relation, start typing and it lists the notes it can point at.
  • From the keyboard: ↓ ↑ to move through the list, Enter to pick, Enter again to add the filter. Esc closes the list.
  • Type the value as the table shows it: Next Action, next action and next-action are the same status. Anything else is kept as typed, except on a checkbox, which takes only true or false.
  • To leave out several values, add one is not for each: Status is not Archive and Status is not Someday.
  • On a property that holds several values — tags, a multi-select, a relation to several notes — is compares the whole list, so it matches only a note with just that one value. Use contains there.

Group and sub-group

  • Group in the toolbar has Group by, then Then by to split each group again. It works on tables and boards: on a table they're groups and sub-groups, on a board they're columns and lanes.
  • None turns a grouping off. A board always has columns, so its Group by has no None; its Then by does.
  • A property that can hold several values — a multi-select, a relation that allows many — is listed but can't be picked, with the reason: “A note can have several of labels, so it cannot be grouped by it.”
  • Groups come in order: a select's options as the type orders them, numbers by size, dates by day, the rest by name — reversed when the view sorts that property descending. Notes with no value gather under No value, last.
  • A checkbox groups into true (ticked) and false, which holds every note not ticked, including those without the box.

Tables

  • Compact rows. Each note is one short row, named by its title; click the name to peek it — ⌘-click opens it in a new tab, ⌘⇧-click beside. Empty cells are left blank, and statuses show as pills.
  • The header stays put. The column names stay at the top as you scroll, as plain words with no icons; an arrow marks the column the table is sorted by.
  • Long values are cut short with …; hover the cell for the whole value. A relation's names stay on one line the same way, and hovering lists them all.

Google Calendar

DFAI can connect to your Google account so that, once block syncing arrives, your timeblocks show as busy on a calendar of their own. For now you can connect, make that calendar and disconnect.

DFAI only ever asks to see and change calendars it made itself — never your main calendar or anyone else's. You sign in in your own browser, and the sign-in is kept in your Mac's Keychain, never in the vault. Nothing in DFAI, and nothing that talks to it, can read the sign-in back.

Before you start: a client ID

Google needs to know which app is asking. Make one in your own Google Cloud project, once:

  1. Turn on the Google Calendar API for the project.
  2. On the OAuth consent screen, add yourself and the scope …/auth/calendar.app.created.
  3. Under Credentials, create an OAuth client ID of type Desktop app. Copy the client ID (it ends in .apps.googleusercontent.com), and the client secret if Google shows one.

Connect

  1. Settings (⌘,) → Google Calendar. Paste the client ID into OAuth client ID, and the secret, if Google gave you one, into Client secret. Press Connect.
  2. Your browser opens Google's sign-in. Pick your account and press Allow. Once DFAI has kept the sign-in, the tab says DFAI is connected to Google Calendar; close it. If it couldn't, the tab says DFAI was not connected and Settings says why. While DFAI waits it says Finish signing in in your browser… — Cancel stops waiting.
  3. Back in Settings it says Connected. Press Create “DFAI blocks” to make the calendar blocks will go to. If you'd made it before, choose it in Calendar for blocks instead. It then says Blocks go to DFAI blocks. A calendar made when the app was called Atlas, Atlas blocks, counts as its calendar too: choose it in Calendar for blocks, and DFAI makes no second one.

The client ID and the calendar are saved in the vault's settings, so your other Mac uses the same calendar. Each Mac signs in for itself.

Disconnect

Press Disconnect…, then Disconnect. DFAI forgets the sign-in on this Mac and asks Google to cancel it. The calendar and its events stay on your Google account. If Google can't be reached, DFAI still forgets it and tells you to remove DFAI yourself from your Google Account's third-party access.

If another vault on this Mac is connected with the same client ID, DFAI forgets this vault's sign-in but doesn't ask Google to cancel it, because that could disconnect the other vault too. Settings says so; disconnect the other vault as well to end DFAI's access.

Queries

Ask across several types at once — tasks and projects together — follow a link to the note it points at, and sort, group and sub-group the answer. Build it from dropdowns or write it as text; both edit the same query.

  • Open New query from the + next to Views, or from search (⌘K).
  • The page opens on DFAI query, already listing every note of your first type. SQL, the other option beside it, is the SQL page as before.
  • Edit the query as Builder or Text. In the builder, the query it makes is shown as text above the dropdowns.

The builder

  • What it should be fits the field: is, is not, contains, starts with, is empty, is not empty; numbers add is less than, is at most and so on; dates read is before, is on or after…
  • The value fits the field too: a select's options, the notes a relation can point at (people for an owner), your tags, ticked or not ticked for a checkbox, and for a date either a date that moves — Today, Yesterday, Tomorrow, A week ago, A week from now, A month from now, Start of this week — or On a day… and a date picker. Switching between builder and text

  • Switch to Text at any time and carry on typing. Switch back and the builder reads the text again.
  • Text you type is kept as you typed it; the builder's own spelling replaces it only when you change something in the builder.
  • The builder shows conditions that are all joined by AND, or all by OR, each plain or NOT. A query that brackets AND with OR, or puts NOT over a group, stays text, and says why: “This query brackets conditions together — AND with OR, or NOT over a group — which the builder cannot show as one list. Edit it as text.” A query with a mistake in it stays text too, with “Fix the query to open it in the builder: …”
  • SHOW has no dropdown; a SHOW you wrote in text is kept when you go back to the builder.
  • LINKS TO this has no dropdown either, so a query with it stays text: “This query says LINKS TO, which the builder has no control for. Edit it as text.” CONNECTED TO this is the same. A condition like people = this goes through the builder unchanged.

Tasks (GTD) and daily notes

Tasks follow GTD: a task is in exactly one of eight statuses, from Inbox to Archive. Capture lands in the Inbox; deciding what a task is moves it on.

StatusWhat it means
InboxCaptured, not yet decided on. Every new task starts here.
BacklogWorth doing, not now — so Next Action stays short.
Next ActionThe next thing to do, ready to be picked up.
In ProgressStarted; it may take days.

A task's properties

  • Waiting on says who a task waits on, and is always optional. Waiting is a status like any other: drag a task there on a board, pick it in the properties panel, or have an automation or Claude set it, and nothing asks who. A status written waiting or [waiting] is Waiting too, and is saved as waiting.
  • Contexts — where it can be done (@phone, @errands); add your own options in Edit type.
  • Defer until — a day before which it stays out of Next actions. Due — when it must be done.
  • Estimate (minutes) — what timeblocks are measured against — Source — where it came from — and Project, filing it under a project or an area.
  • Completed is written for you: the day a task was finished.

Tick it off

  • Every task shows a checkbox in tables, lists, boards, galleries and feeds. Ticking moves it to Archive and writes today as Completed; unticking puts back the status it had and takes the day away. (Unticking after a restart, when DFAI no longer remembers, puts it at Next Action.)
  • A task made already in Archive — from a board's Archive column, say — is given today as Completed too.
  • Recurring tasks roll on to their next date when ticked, or when dragged into Archive on a board, and go back to Next Action.

Checklists and progress

A task that takes days stays In Progress, and its steps are a checklist in its text — - [ ] lines, typed or made with / → Task list.

  • Progress is the share of boxes ticked: a bar and a percentage on its card on a board, its row in a list, and under its name in a table. 2 of 5 ticked is 40%; it reads 100% only when every box is ticked. Nested boxes and boxes in a quote count; a note with no box shows no bar.
  • Ask for it in a query: FROM task WHERE progress >= 50 SORT BY progress DESC, or a project's with project.progress. It is worked out from the file whenever the note is saved, so rebuilding the index gives the same numbers. A type with a Progress property of its own keeps that instead, and draws no bar.

Make a line its own task

When a step grows into a task of its own, click into its line and press Make task after its words, or ⇧⌘↩.

  • A task named by the line is made in Tasks/<year>/<month>, its home folder, in the Inbox — or Archive, with today as Completed, if the line was ticked. Its Source links back to the line, and its Project is the note's project (or the note itself, when it is a project or an area).
  • Nothing the line said is lost. A line of plain words becomes a link to the task that reads the same — Order chairs stays Order chairs, even when the file had to be named differently. A line with a link, a tag, code or bold in it keeps all of that after the new link, and the new task starts with the line in its text too, so the person it mentions and its tags are linked from the task as well.
  • The line keeps its box, and the lines under it stay. The list it is in is written again, so a list written with * or numbers comes back with - — the same as typing in it would. The rest of the note is untouched.
  • A line that is a task already — it starts with a link to one — isn't made a task again: That line is the task “Order chairs” already. A very long line's task gets a name cut short to fit a file name.
  • The box at the bottom right says “Order chairs” is a task now. with Open and Undo; it goes by itself after 5 seconds, and waits while the pointer or the focus is on it. Undo puts the line back exactly as it was and sends the task to the Trash, in one step — unless either changed since, which a line under the page's bar says until you dismiss it. So does a line that could not be made a task. ⌘Z in the note doesn't take a promotion back: a note read again from its file is never a step to undo.
  • Typing in the note is saved first. A line that is empty can't be made a task, and a template's lines aren't offered.

The GTD views

  • Inbox — tasks still to decide on. Next actions — what can be done now, leaving out anything deferred to a later day until that day comes. Waiting — grouped by who it waits on. Someday and Longterm — a board of the two, to drag between.
  • They are ordinary query views in .atlas/views, added when your tasks move to GTD. A view of yours with the same name is never replaced.

Today

Today at the top of the sidebar — or ⌘K → Today — is the morning page: what needs you today, on one screen. It shows once your vault has tasks, and needs no saved view. Each heading carries its count, and a task is listed once, in the first section that claims it — so an In Progress task due yesterday is under Overdue only, and the counts add up.

  • Overdue — still open and due before today, the earliest first. Due today — due today. Both are only said of open work: Someday and Longterm promise no day.
  • Back from deferral — tasks whose Defer until is today, in any status but Archive (Someday and Longterm included): what you put off until now. An Inbox, Backlog, Someday or Longterm task whose day has already passed is listed here too — nothing else on the page would show it — and stays until you deal with it or put it off again. Each says the day it was deferred until.
  • In progress — what is under way.
  • Next actions — grouped by project, each project's name opening it, with No project last.
  • Waiting — grouped by who it waits on, the longest wait first, each with its days: Quote from Larkspur — 4 days since last change. The days are counted from the task's file last changing, in whole days — DFAI keeps no “waiting since”, so any edit to the task starts its count again.

A task deferred to a later day is in no section until that day. Above the sections: the Inbox with its count, Stalled projects — active projects with nothing next, which opens the weekly review — and Open today's note (⇧⌘D). The day is your Mac's own date, and turns over at midnight where you are: a page left open overnight shows the new day within a minute, or as soon as you look at the window again.

Each row shows the task's project, contexts and due date, opens from its name, and has:

  • A done box — ticks the task as a type's view does: finished and dated today, or a repeating task rolled on to its next date.
  • Move to… any other status, Waiting included — it never asks who.
  • +1d and +7d — defer until tomorrow, or for a week. The task leaves the page and comes back that day.

The page updates in place. A row acts on what it showed you: if the task has changed since the page read it — ticked in another view, say — the action is refused and says so, and nothing is written. A row you have just acted on takes no other action until the page has caught up. The box at the bottom right offers Undo for the last thing you did — it puts back exactly what the action changed, as it was written, and refuses if the task has been edited since. Everything is reachable with Tab; after a row leaves, focus lands on the row that was under it, so Space ticks down a list.

If your vault has a saved view titled Today, the sidebar still shows one Today row — the page — and the page links to your view from its top right: Open the “Today” view.

The weekly review

Weekly review in the sidebar, under the Inbox — or ⌘K → Weekly review — puts GTD's questions of the week on one page:

  • Inbox — how many wait in the Inbox, counted as the sidebar counts them: notes and tasks to process and proposals to answer — and Open the Inbox.
  • Waiting for more than 7 days — Waiting tasks nobody has touched in over a week, the longest first, with who they wait on — a linked person or a name you typed: Quote from Larkspur — On Mara Quill.
  • Active projects with no next action — a project whose status is active with no task filed under it at Next Action or In Progress.
  • Overdue — tasks due before today that are still open: Inbox, Backlog, Next Action, In Progress or Waiting. Someday and Longterm promise no day, so they are never overdue.
  • Someday and Longterm, untouched for 30 days — ideas left alone for over a month, the oldest first.

“Untouched” means the file hasn't changed: DFAI keeps no “waiting since”, so any edit — yours, or the review's own — counts as a look. A task deferred to a later day is in no section until that day, as it is out of Next actions. Finished and archived notes are never listed. Whether a project has a next action is asked of the whole vault, so it is right even in a vault too big for the review to read every task.

Each item opens from its name, and has quick actions. Acting on one takes it out of its section:

  • Move to… another status — only ones that take it out of its section. A waiting task or an old idea can go to any status but Archive; a late task only to Someday or Longterm, since in any other open status it would still be late (or Defer it, or Archive it). A project can go to any of its type's statuses — paused, say.
  • Defer a task a week: Defer until becomes seven days from today, and the task comes back then.
  • Archive a task to finish it, as ticking it does: today becomes Completed, or a repeating task rolls on to its next date, back at Next Action. A project goes into the Archive, as its Archive command does.

Move your tasks to GTD

A vault whose tasks use other statuses — this project's own backlog, next, doing, review, done, say — is offered the move on the Inbox page: “Tasks follow GTD's eight statuses, from Inbox to Archive. 85 tasks would move to them.”

  1. Press Preview the move. Nothing is written yet.
  2. Check the mapping. Each status your tasks hold is listed with how many tasks hold it and what it becomes: backlog → Backlog, next → Next Action, doing and review → In Progress, done → Archive, anything else → Inbox. Change any of them from its menu. A task the mapping sends to Waiting goes there whether or not Waiting on names anyone.
  3. Read the rest: what changes in the Task type (its status becomes the eight; the GTD properties it lacks are added, and properties you added stay), views and automations rewritten (Roadmap: done → archive), any listed for you to change by hand — a filter like status contains “do” that no one status can stand for, status != review (review and doing both become In Progress, so leaving it out would leave out doing too), a status nobody knew, a query or automation over tasks and another type (a project's done is not a task's), or a SQL view — the GTD views to be added, and every task, old status → new. A finished task is given the day its file last changed as Completed.
  4. Press Move N tasks. Only the files in the preview are written, each in its status and Completed alone — the rest of every file stays byte for byte. A task with unsaved typing in DFAI is left as it is, and said so.
  • Undo the move to GTD is offered on the Inbox page for a week after the move, or until you press Dismiss beside it; then it is gone for good, after a restart and on your other Mac too. Moving more tasks later offers it again, for that later move alone: its Undo never takes back a move whose undo you dismissed or that finished before it.
  • Undo the move to GTD puts back each file's properties exactly as they were, and takes away the views it added; a file nobody touched since is back byte for byte. A file whose properties changed since the move is left as it is, and said so in Activity; one whose text alone changed gets its properties back and keeps the new text.
  • The move and its undo are each a line in Activity, with every file left alone and why. Nothing about it is silent.
  • If the move stops partway — the Mac sleeps, a disk refuses a file — open the Inbox again: the preview lists only what is left, and one undo covers both runs — keeping anything you changed in between. Running it again when nothing is left changes nothing, and once the move is done the Inbox no longer reads your tasks to check — unless a file was left or a GTD view is missing.
  • The record undo works from is kept in .atlas/migrations, so it syncs and works from either Mac.

Today's note

  • Today's note ⇧⌘D — or + beside the vault's name → Today's note — opens the day's note, making it if needed. It is named by the date, in its year's folder — Daily/2026/2026-10-12.md — whichever note you're on. One made at the top of the vault before is still found and opened.
  • A new one starts from your Daily template. With none, it starts as a note of the Daily type with two headings, What happened and What is next. A Daily template that makes a task is held to the task rules as any new task is — one made already in Archive is dated.
  • Daily notes you already have — the Notion import writes them as 2026-10-12.md at the top, type: daily — are opened as they are: never made again, numbered or rewritten.
  • Daily is a built-in type. A vault without it is offered it on the Inbox page; until you add it, daily notes still open and say type: daily. Once added, the Daily type's own page lists every day.

Quick capture from any app

  • Quick capture ⇧⌘N inside DFAI: type a line and it becomes a task in the Inbox, without leaving what you're doing.
  • From any app, press ⌃⌥N. DFAI comes forward — out of the Dock if it was minimised, onto the Space you're on if it was on another — with quick capture open and ready to type in. Return saves it to the Inbox; Esc puts capture away, and ⌘Tab takes you back.
  • No vault open? Nothing is captured, and DFAI says so: “Quick capture needs a vault: open one, and what you capture lands in its Inbox.”
  • With Settings or search up, the press brings DFAI forward and opens nothing over them — close them first, as with ⇧⌘N.
  • Change it in Settings (⌘,) → Quick capture from anywhere: Change…, then press the new keys — hold two of ⌘, ⌥ and ⌃ with a letter, digit, function key (F1–F20) or Space. One alone is refused: a shortcut from any app reaches DFAI first, so ⌘C would stop copying everywhere and ⌥E typing é. So are macOS's own, such as ⌃⌘Q (Lock Screen) and ⌥⌘Esc (Force Quit). Turn off stops it; Use ⌃⌥N puts the default back. It's set for each Mac on its own.
  • If macOS won't give DFAI the keys, Settings says “— not working” beside them, with why, and the shortcut you had before keeps working: choose others. macOS may also let two apps share a shortcut, and DFAI can't always tell — if a press opens another app, or nothing, choose other keys.
  • It works while DFAI is running. Closing DFAI's window quits it, and then nothing is listening.
  • Inside DFAI, ⌥⌘N, and your capture-from-anywhere keys (⌃⌥N to start with), open capture too. One difference from ⇧⌘N: ⌥⌘N does nothing while a palette, a dialog or the peek is up, where ⇧⌘N still opens capture. With no vault open, none of them opens it: DFAI says it needs a vault first.
  • Today in the sidebar is a view, and Inbox the Inbox page, which lists everything in the Inbox folder, every meeting not yet filed, and every task still at Inbox; their counts update as you work.

Timeblocking

Set time aside on the calendar for your tasks: one block for a big task, or one block that several small tasks are pushed into. Each task shows how much of its estimate is scheduled.

Blocks

  • A block is a note of the Block type: Start and End, each a day and a time (2026-10-12T09:00), and Tasks, the tasks it holds. Make one like any note of a type, with New block on the Block type's page.
  • DFAI adds the Block type once your tasks follow GTD — when the vault opens, or as soon as you move your tasks — and says so in Activity. A Block type you already have is left as it is, and it can't be deleted — its properties are yours to change.
  • A block also keeps the Google Calendar event it will sync with (Google event, Google version); leave them alone.

Plan the day

Open the Block type, add a view with + and choose Calendar: each block sits on the clock from its start to its end. In Week, 3 days or Day, your Next actions stand beside the clock, the ones with no time set aside yet at the top, each with 1h of 2h scheduled under its name.

  • Drag a task onto empty time to make a block for it, starting on the quarter hour you let go in and as long as the task still needs — its estimate less what its blocks already give it (half an hour if it has no estimate, or already has all it needs; a day at most). The block is named after the task — Quarterly report block — and uses your Block template if you have one.
  • Drag a task onto a block to add it to the block's tasks. The block then shares its time among them, as the table below says. A block that already holds the task is left as it is.
  • Split a task by dragging it again: the next block is sized to what is left. Shorten the first block from its bottom edge, and the next drag fills the rest.
  • Undo, beside the last drop, takes it back: a block it made goes to the Trash — unless you've changed it since, or are typing in it, which DFAI tells you and leaves — and a task it added to a block comes out again.
  • Keyboard: Tab to a task and press Return (or click it). Focus goes to 9:00 today, or on the first day shown; ↑ ↓ move through the hours and Tab to the next day, or to a block. Return places the task there; Esc puts it back.
  • Press Esc during a drag to cancel it: nothing is written. The clock doesn't scroll while you drag a task, so scroll to the part of the day first.

Scheduled, estimate and done

A task's page shows, under its properties, Scheduled — 1h 30m of 2h — and Done — 0m of 2h. Both follow from the blocks and the status, so there is nothing to type.

The block holdsWhat each task gets
One taskThe whole block, whatever its estimate.
Several tasks that need the whole block or moreA share in proportion to what each has left of its estimate. An hour with three 20-minute tasks gives each 20m.
Several tasks that need lessWhat each has left; the rest goes in equal parts to the tasks with no estimate, or stays free.
  • Split a big task by putting it in several blocks: a 2-hour task in blocks of 1h, 30m and 30m reads 2h of 2h.
  • Over-scheduled: more time than the estimate says so — 30m over the estimate. Two blocks that overlap both count, so a task in both shows up this way.
  • Done is all of the estimate once the task is finished (in Archive), none before. A finished task has nothing left, so a block of several gives it no more time.
  • Estimate is in minutes. A vault that keeps its own text estimates can write 2h or 1h30m; a day or a week (1d) is read as no estimate, since only you know how many hours your day holds. A task with no estimate shows the time scheduled alone.
  • Times are read as written, on the wall clock. A block from 23:45 to 00:15 is half an hour, into the next day; one across a daylight-saving change is as long as the clock says. A block without a time at both ends, or ending before it starts, gives no time to anything.

Links and the graph

  • Click a link to peek the note it names, over the page you are reading. ⌘-click opens it in a new tab and ⌘⇧-click beside; a link to a heading or a block opens its note there. Rest the pointer on a link for a moment to see its card.
  • Link a note by typing [[ and the start of its name — the list ranks notes as a link field does (see Properties); if no note is called what you typed, Create ‘…’ writes the link and makes the note beside the one you're in, as Obsidian would. A name with /, | or # is never offered, and one a file can't hold is cleaned — [[Q3: plan makes Q3 plan and links [[Q3 plan]]. ⌘Z takes the link back out but leaves the note it made; delete that like any note. Link a heading or block with [[Note# (see Blocks and embeds), a person by typing @ (see People and mentions), with / → Link to note, or … → Link to…. Any link to a note can also show as a card — see Bookmarks.
  • The Links panel at the foot of a note shows links in and out, including relations like a task's project; on a person's page, the links in are headed Mentioned in. Unlinked mentions finds notes that name this one in plain text; Link turns the mention into a link.
  • Graph (under Search) draws notes as dots coloured by type, sized by how connected they are; relations are dashed. Pan, zoom, drag; click to open, ⌘-click to open beside.
  • … → Show in graph centres it on one note, one or two steps out. Filter by type, hide unlinked notes, or switch to List for the keyboard.
  • Archived notes aren't drawn, and links from them don't count, until you turn on the graph's Archived switch.

Bookmarks

A bookmark is a link to a note shown as a card: the note's picture, its title, a line or two of what it says, and where it lives. It is still an ordinary [[link]] in the file, so Obsidian reads it as one.

Turn a link into a card

From the link

Hover a [[link]] and click the … above it (Link options), or right-click the link, or put the caret on it and press ⇧F10 or the menu key. Choose Show as bookmark.

While linking

In the [[ list, ⇧ Return or a Shift-click puts the note in as a card. The list's foot shows ↵ Link, ⇧ ↵ Bookmark.

  • A link in the middle of a sentence takes its paragraph apart: the words before stay a paragraph, the card follows, the words after go below it.
  • To go back, click the card's … (Options for <title>) → Show as link. You get the link alone in a paragraph.
  • The same menu has Open note.

What the card shows

  • Picture: the note's thumbnail — the one you chose, or the picture of its page once DFAI has made it — else its cover or first image, else an icon. See Thumbnails.
  • Title, as the note's page shows it.
  • Summary: the note's description property, then its summary, then its first paragraph, as plain words. A long one is cut with an ellipsis.
  • Where it lives: the note's folder, and an Archived chip if it's in the Archive.
  • A link to a note that isn't there shows No note is called “X” — it may have been renamed or deleted. A note that can't be read shows This note could not be read just now.
  • [[#Heading]], a link to a heading in the same note, makes a card of that same note.
  • Cards refresh when the note they point to changes.

Use one

  • Click the card to peek its note; ⌘-click opens it in a new tab.
  • With a card selected, Return starts a new line after it and ⌘ Return opens the note.

Where cards can go

Cards live only at the top level of a note. Links inside lists, quotes and tables aren't offered Show as bookmark, and a card pasted into a quote, callout, list or table goes in as its plain link.

In the file. A bookmark is the link alone on its line with a marker after it: [[Note]] <!-- atlas:bookmark -->. Obsidian's reading view and GitHub hide the comment; Obsidian's live preview may show it dimmed while the caret is on that line. A plain [[Note]] on its own line stays a link.

Not yet: web links don't become cards. A web link with the marker is kept as written.

Blocks and embeds

Link to one paragraph, list item, task or heading of a note, or show it inside another note, live. DFAI writes these the way Obsidian does, so they work in both.

Link a block

  • Type ![[ and pick a note. Its headings and blocks are offered next, each with a line of preview and its kind (Heading 2, Text, List item, Task, Quote, Callout, Code, Table); typing narrows them. [[Note# does the same for a plain link.
  • Picking writes ![[Note#^id]] for a block or ![[Note#Heading]] for a heading.
  • If the block has no id yet, DFAI adds one (six letters and digits) to its note and changes nothing else there. While that note has unsaved typing it refuses with The note has unsaved changes. Save it, then pick again.

The embed

  • ![[Note#^id]] or ![[Note#Heading]] alone on its line shows that block, live and read-only, under its note's name. Change the source and the embed follows.
  • Click the name (Open the block where it is written) to open the note at the block, briefly highlighted. A [[Note#^id]] link does the same.
  • A missing note shows No note is called “X” — it may have been renamed or deleted. A missing block, “Note” has no block ^id — it may have been deleted., or “Note” has no heading “H” any more. One that can't be read, This block could not be read just now.
  • A block from an archived note still shows, with an Archived chip.
  • One level deep: an embed inside a shown block is shown as a link. A note can embed its own blocks too.
  • ![[file.pdf#…]] and other embeds of things that aren't notes stay links.

Block ids

  • Obsidian's ^id, at the end of a paragraph, list item, task or heading, or on its own line after a table, quote, callout or code block. DFAI shows it as a faint ^id after the words.
  • An id follows its block through edits, and when the block becomes a list item, task, quote or heading.
  • A note that already has the same id twice keeps both; when you pick one of them, it gets a new id of its own so the link opens the block you chose.
  • Renaming the source note keeps #^id and #Heading on every link to it.

Not yet: editing an embedded block in place. Open it in its note and edit it there.

People and mentions

Type @ and a name to link a person. A person is a note of the Person type; the mention is an ordinary link, so Obsidian reads it too.

Mention someone

  • @ and a letter lists people: every note with type: person, archived ones left out. A name that matches exactly comes first, then the people you edited most recently. ↓ ↑ choose; Return or a click picks.
  • Picking writes [[Name]] — or the note's path, like [[People/Name]], when another note shares the name — and draws it as a chip with the person's initial. The file keeps plain [[Name]].
  • @ starts a mention at the start of a line or after a space, (, a non-breaking space or an opening quote. Not straight after a letter or digit, so an email like ann@example.com stays as typed, and not in code or inside a link.
  • Names with dots work: @J.R. Hartley finds J.R. Hartley.

Add a new person

  • For a name of up to three words, the list ends with Create person “…”. Return alone never makes one — when it is the only choice, Return is just a new line. Press ↓ to it, then Return, or click it.
  • The new note goes in People/, made from the Person template, numbered if the name is taken there. While it's being made a faded @Name holds its place, then becomes the link.
  • If you change the note before they're made, the link isn't written, and a message says so: “Ann Lee was added to People, but not linked: the note changed while they were being made.”
  • DFAI won't make a person whose note would change what an existing [[link]] opens, or whose name holds # [ ] | or ^, since a link stops there. People who already have such a name are listed greyed out with the reason, like “A|B” cannot be linked: a link stops at |.

Mentioned in

A person's page shows their meetings, what you're waiting on them for, and their open tasks and decisions — see Linked pages. On a person's page the Links panel heads the notes linking to them Mentioned in. Only a note whose type is exactly person counts as a person — for @ and for this heading.

Other spellings of a name

A Person — and a Company — has an Also spelt property (aliases in the file): a nickname or a short form, commas between them in the properties panel. They are for matching a meeting's attendees to the person; DFAI never uses them to correct text. To correct a mishearing of the name, list it under Misheard as (variants) on the same note — or add the name on the Terms page, which puts it there.

A vault whose Person or Company type was made before this has no such property yet: add one called aliases on Edit type as a Multi-select, or give the note an aliases list by hand. DFAI reads aliases either way.

Merge two people into one

The same person can end up as two notes — mara@larkspur.example.com and mara.quill@larkspur.example.com made two, or Tobias Fenn.md sits at the top of the vault and in People/. On the one to get rid of, open the page's … menu (or the right sidebar's Properties) and choose Merge into…, then pick the person to keep.

To merge several at once, tick them in the Person table (or in a People view with Select on) and choose Merge 2 people into… on the bar above it — or ⌘K Merge selected people… — then pick the person they all go into.

Before anything is written, DFAI says what the merge does: how many links in how many notes will open the kept person, the aliases and emails they gain, and any value the two disagree on — the kept person keeps their own, and the other is listed so you can copy it by hand. Merge then:

  • points every link to the other person at the kept one — in text, embeds, properties like a meeting's people, your templates and the filters of saved views — keeping its display text, #heading and ^block;
  • adds their emails to Other emails and their name and aliases to Also spelt, so the next meeting with them finds the kept person;
  • joins lists (projects, tags…) and types, and fills properties the kept person had empty;
  • adds their text at the end, under Merged from …;
  • puts their note in the Trash, where you can get it back in Finder.

A toast says how it went, with a link to Activity. There is no Undo: the Trash keeps the other note. Before the preview DFAI saves what you've typed anywhere and catches the index up, so a link you've just written is found. A link in one of DFAI's own files other than a template, view or dashboard (a type, say), or a view that names the person by file in its SQL, stops the merge until you change it by hand; so does typing DFAI couldn't save. If a note changed after the preview, nothing is merged and DFAI asks you to look again. If a write fails part-way, the toast names exactly which notes were changed and which weren't, nobody goes to the Trash, and merging again picks up where it stopped.

Find people who may be one

On the Person type's page, Possible duplicates (also in ⌘K as Possible duplicate people) lists pairs that look like one person: the same name in two folders, the same email, an alias that is the other's name, or emails that match — mara and mara.quill at one company, mara.quill and maraquill, a typo like marra.quill, or a domain a letter off. At public mail providers (Gmail, Outlook, iCloud…) only the same address written with or without dots counts. Each pair shows why, with Merge… (and a button to keep the other one instead) or Not the same, which DFAI remembers in the vault's settings so the pair isn't offered again.

A meeting's attendee also matches a person whose Also spelt holds their email address.

Waiting for, and how long

  • Each waiting-for says how long it has waited — waiting 12 days — longest first.
  • The count runs from when the task last changed, the same measure the weekly review uses: DFAI keeps no "waiting since" date, so editing the task starts the count again.
  • A waiting-for is listed once. On a person's page it is under Waiting for, not again under Open tasks.

Open tasks

  • Grouped by status with what to do first on top: Next Action, In Progress, Inbox, Backlog, then Someday and Longterm. Finished tasks are left out. Click a status to fold it.
  • An active project with open tasks and none at Next Action or In Progress says so over the list: “No next action: none of its 3 open tasks is Next Action or In Progress.” It is the weekly review's rule, so a paused or finished project, one with no status, and an area never say it. Nor does a project with more than 200 open tasks, where the list was cut.

Meetings and tasks, both ways

  • A meeting lists the tasks and decisions linked with it, and each of those shows the meeting under Meetings. Any link between the two counts, whichever note holds it: the task's source citing a line of the meeting, a meeting property, the meeting listing the task, or a link in either one's text.
  • So the tasks that came from a meeting are there, and so is a task that merely mentions it — and a task you make by hand joins a meeting the moment either links the other.

What decides the sections

  • The page's type, and what your types declare. A person's meetings are the ones whose People relation holds them; if your Meeting type has no relation to Person, any link from the meeting counts instead.
  • Waiting for needs a Task type with a Waiting status and a Waiting on relation — see Move your tasks to GTD. Without them the page shows open tasks only.
  • Linked notes lists notes of your other types, and what a section's own type links outside its property. A note with no type isn't there — it is under Links at the foot of the page, as before.
  • A company's Meetings are the ones whose Companies holds it. A meeting only its people were in isn't there.
  • A template shows no sections: it isn't a page of its type.
  • To list something else on one page, add a query block; it sits in the note's text beside these.

Meetings

Your meeting notes come into the vault by themselves. A meeting is a note of the Meeting type, one markdown file per meeting, with the transcript split into turns you can link to and quote.

How a meeting arrives

Gemini's meetings come as below. Granola's are pulled by DFAI itself — see Meetings from Granola.

  1. The meeting ends and Google Meet's “Notes by Gemini” email lands, as it always has.
  2. n8n, on your work computer, reads the email and sends the meeting two places from the same step: to Notion's Meeting Notes, as before, and to DFAI's Local API (POST /v1/meetings) on the Mac.
  3. DFAI writes it into the vault you have open, as one markdown file in its month, Meetings/<year>/<month>/<date> <title>.md — for example Meetings/2026/09/2026-09-29 Platform weekly sync.md. A second meeting with the same name that day goes beside it as … (gemini 1a2b3c4d).md. This needs DFAI open, with the Local API on.

DFAI never logs in to Gmail, Notion or the notetaker; n8n hands it the meeting. The two sends don't wait on each other: if Notion's write fails, DFAI still gets the meeting, and if DFAI can't take it, Notion still has it. n8n tries DFAI three times, five seconds apart, then emails you “This meeting did not reach DFAI” with why — usually DFAI was closed or the Local API off. Open it and re-run that execution in n8n. Sending a meeting again is safe: if the vault already has it, DFAI says so and writes nothing. DFAI checks a meeting against the import contract before writing it, so one it would refuse is never written. A meeting with no start DFAI can trust — no time in the email's subject, and no transcript length to count back from when the notes arrived — is refused, never guessed.

What DFAI does when it arrives

Once the file has arrived, DFAI checks it against the meeting import contract (the same check as pnpm validate:meeting below). It then writes one line saying what it did, atlas_import_outcome:, right under the file's atlas_import: meeting/v1 line, and does one of three things. Each is one line in Activity, under Meetings.

It's fine — imported

That line is the only thing the import writes; the rest of the file is as it came. DFAI then links it to who was there. It stays in its month folder and shows in the Inbox until you file it under a project or an area, or archive it. Activity: “2026-09-29 Platform weekly sync: arrived in the Inbox.”

It's a second copy — duplicate

Same provider and provider's id as a meeting already in the vault, wherever it is, even one you filed or archived. The copy goes to the Archive and is marked there with a link to the one kept, by its path: atlas_duplicate_of: "[[Meetings/2026/09/2026-09-29 Platform weekly sync]]". Activity: “… a second copy of 2026-09-29 Platform weekly sync, so it was archived.”

A meeting already imported is always the one kept. Of two that arrive together, the one at <date> <title>.md is kept over the one written at … (gemini 1a2b3c4d).md because that name was taken.

Something's wrong with it — error

It stays where it landed. Nothing in it is fixed or deleted. DFAI writes at the top why it failed, the first few problems with line numbers:

atlas_import_error: "external_id is required; Transcript: Line 15: the turn has no block id — end it with ^t0001 and so on"

It shows in the Inbox with that error beside it (and as Import error in its properties). Activity gets a warning: “2026-10-02 Vendor call: could not be imported. external_id is required”.

  • Fix it in place. Correct the file in DFAI and save. DFAI checks it again, takes atlas_import_error out, and stamps it imported. Activity: “… now follows the import contract, so its import error was taken out.” To see every problem, not just the first few, run pnpm validate:meeting on it.
  • Or send it again. Fix what n8n sends, then re-run that execution. A file with an import error doesn't count as having the meeting, so the new copy is written beside it, at … (gemini 1a2b3c4d).md. Delete the broken file first to keep the meeting's own name.
  • Want the copy back? Unarchive it. It keeps its duplicate stamp, and DFAI leaves it alone.
  • Your edits are yours. Once a file has its imported or duplicate line, DFAI never looks at it again. Editing it, adding your own sections, renaming it or filing it under a project is never undone, marked or archived, and it is still the meeting a later copy is matched against. Keep the line: take it out and DFAI sees the file as just arrived.
  • A sync conflict. If both Macs changed a meeting before they synced, sync keeps this Mac's version and puts the other beside it as … (conflict from Mac).md. DFAI never imports or archives that copy. Activity warns you about it once while DFAI is open (again only if the copy changes): compare the two, keep what you want, and delete the copy. A file DFAI can't read or write is said once the same way.
  • One Mac does it. Only the Mac that runs your automations imports meetings. Any other Mac leaves them alone. After you move the automations to the other Mac, both may import until the first Mac next syncs and reads that: they come to the same answer for every file, and sync keeps both if they write the same file.
  • It waits for a pull first. With sync on, after DFAI starts or the Mac wakes, nothing is imported until a sync (or a look at GitHub that finds nothing new) has finished, and only if the settings it brought still name this Mac. So a Mac that was asleep while the other took over finds out before it writes anything. A meeting file that changed on this Mac after the last pull, like one this Mac's own Granola pull just wrote, waits for the next pull, a minute or so by default. DFAI checks again right before it writes each file.
  • Paused or unreadable, it waits. While sync is paused on this Mac, nothing is imported; Settings → Sync and Activity say so. If .atlas/settings.md can't be read (missing after a pull, or broken), nothing is imported until it can be; Activity says that too.
  • After each pull, once its index is ready, the importing Mac settles every meeting file without the line: each in a month folder that says atlas_import: meeting/v1, and every file in Inbox/Meetings. That includes files that came while it was closed or asleep, and files it couldn't finish (you were typing in it, or its frontmatter wouldn't read); Activity says which. Hidden files there are left alone, as everywhere in DFAI.
  • Only those are checked. A meeting you make yourself in a month folder is just a note, and no import line is written into it. So is a meeting filed some other way, like Meetings/From Notion. Inbox/Meetings is still read, file by file: an n8n workflow you haven't re-imported keeps writing there.
Your own vault's Inbox. Meetings are imported in any vault. Whether the Inbox lists them depends on the vault's Inbox view and Meeting type, which DFAI doesn't change for you yet. The vault in the DFAI repository has both. In yours, copy its .atlas/views/Inbox.md over your Inbox view. Then, in Edit type, add three properties to your Meeting type: atlas_import_outcome (select: imported, duplicate, error; “Import”), atlas_import_error (text, “Import error”) and atlas_duplicate_of (relation to Meeting, “Duplicate of”). Until then, the files are in their month folders under Meetings/, and Activity lists every one.

Misheard names, put right

As a meeting is let in, DFAI puts right the spellings your Terms say it mishears — and only those: a term's, a person's or a company's Misheard as. A person's or company's Also spelt is for linking people and is never used to change text. Say you have the term Larkspur Pay, misheard as Lark Spur Pay, and the term Mara Quill, misheard as Mara Quil: a turn reading “**Mara Quil** … Lark Spur Pay is ready.” arrives as “**Mara Quill** … Larkspur Pay is ready.”

  • Only the Summary, Notes, Provider next steps and Transcript are corrected; the title and anything above the first section are left as they came.
  • Whole words, exactly as the term writes them — capitals included. A variant Lark Spur Pay corrects Lark  Spur Pay (any run of spaces, never across a line break) and Lark Spur Pay's keeps its 's; lark spur pay, LARK SPUR PAY and Lark Spur Payroll are not touched. So a common word is safe: a variant Pay never changes pay. If both lark spur and Lark Spur turn up, list both. A name already spelt right is left exactly as written, even when a line break or emphasis falls inside it (**Mara** Quill is not turned into Mara Mara Quill), and a spelling two notes disagree about (a conflict on the Terms page) is never corrected.
  • One thing at a time. A word joined to another by -, ., /, @ or \ — an email, a web address, a file path, quill-based — is left alone, and C# is not found inside C#sharp. Where two misheard spellings run into each other (Lark Spur and Spur Pay in Lark Spur Pay), only the first is put right; list the whole Lark Spur Pay too to get both words.
  • Never inside a [[link]], a markdown link or URL, a #tag, code or a fenced code block, a comment, or a ^block-id.
  • Recorded on the meeting. Its frontmatter gets atlas_corrections: — each change, with from, to, count, the blocks (turn and paragraph ids) it was made in, and at, where each one stands — and two checks, atlas_corrections_digest: of the body as corrected and atlas_corrections_original_digest: of the body as it was. Activity's line for the meeting says how many: “2026-09-29 Platform weekly sync: arrived in the Inbox. 2 misheard spellings corrected; Undo corrections on the meeting puts them back.”
  • A meeting with nothing misheard is not changed. If you have terms with misheard spellings, the same write adds atlas_corrections: none so the meeting is never read again; if you have none yet, nothing is added, so terms you add later still apply.

Undo corrections

A corrected meeting's page has a band under its bar: Corrected · 2 spellings put right on import, then each change, the old spelling struck through — Lark Spur Pay → Larkspur Pay ×2. Undo corrections puts every original word back, byte for byte, and sets atlas_corrections: undone, so the band goes and the meeting is never corrected again. Claude's summary at the top of the meeting doesn't count as an edit: Undo works around it and leaves it as it is. If the meeting was edited since, Undo says so and turns itself off; Dismiss hides the band for now (it comes back when the meeting is opened again).

  • Refused if the meeting's text changed since, as an automation's Undo is: “The meeting changed since its words were corrected, so they were left as they are.” Putting the old words back into text you've since edited could undo your edit. Changing its properties — a tag, a project — doesn't count. Anything you've typed is saved first, so it counts.
  • An atlas_corrections list edited by hand into something DFAI didn't write is refused too, and nothing is changed.
  • Corrections are read through the Local API as the meeting's properties (GET /v1/notes/{path}). Undo stays in the app: it rewrites a note you're reading.

Who was there: people, companies and the project

As a meeting is let in, DFAI links it, so clicking a person or a company shows their meetings. The links, the corrected spellings and the imported line all arrive together: an automation set off when a meeting is created runs once, on the meeting already linked and corrected. What it's sure of it writes into the meeting; what it isn't sure of waits in the Inbox as a proposal. It never guesses.

  • People, by email first. An attendee whose address is a person's email, or one of their other_emails, is that person — mara@larkspur.example is Mara Quill whatever name the notetaker wrote. With no match by address, DFAI tries the name, then their Also spelt aliases, ignoring case and spacing — the same way the Terms page compares spellings. Everyone it finds is added to the meeting's People.
  • Speakers too. A named speaker in the transcript who is exactly one person is added as well, by the name your Terms correct it to: a turn heard as Tobias Fen, which a term puts right, links Tobias Fenn. You and unknown speakers are left alone.
  • Companies, by email domain. An attendee at larkspur.example links Larkspur Payroll when the company lists that domain in Email domains (or its Site, when that is the site's front page rather than a page below it). If two of your companies list domains that both fit, the more specific one wins, so a subsidiary isn't taken for its parent. Gmail, Outlook and the like never count, in any country, and neither do sites like YouTube, LinkedIn or yourco.zendesk.com. A person's own Company is linked too. Two companies claiming one domain at the same depth link neither.
  • A company only a domain's name points at is proposed, not linked. If fenn.example looks like a company called “Fenn” that lists no domain of its own, it waits in the Inbox as a link proposal on the meeting's Companies for you to accept.
  • Someone new is proposed, not made. An attendee DFAI can't find waits in the Inbox as a Person proposal with their name, email and the company their address is at. Accept makes them in People/ and adds them to the meeting's people: the card says “And adds it to people of 2026-10-06 Payroll sync.” If you already accepted them from another meeting, accepting here just links them; if you filed the meeting since, it's still found — by its id, not its old place. If it has been archived instead, DFAI says so and writes nothing. Reject someone once and they aren't proposed again from later meetings.
  • Two people with one name or alias — two Tobys — is never a guess: each is proposed as a link on the meeting, low confidence. Accept the one it was and reject the other.
  • Group addresses are never people. payroll-team@larkspur.example stays in the attendees and is never proposed. Neither are you: an attendee with your profile name — however it's written, even “Last, First” — or one of Your emails is left out. If the one person of an attendee's name has other addresses on file, DFAI proposes the link instead of making it.
  • The likely project. If the meeting has no project, DFAI proposes the open project most tied to its people and company — one they own, link, or met about before — and names the runners-up on the card. It's always a proposal; only you set a meeting's project.
  • Once per meeting. DFAI links a meeting as it's let in, and never again: take someone out of its people and they stay out. It only ever adds — nothing already linked is changed — and a proposal you rejected isn't made again. A meeting you're typing in is left alone, and Activity says so: “2026-10-06 Payroll sync: linked Mara Quill, Larkspur Payroll; proposed 1 in the Inbox.”

What Claude pulls out

Turn on the ready-made automation Pull actions and decisions out of a new meeting (open Automations, click the button of that name, then Create automation) and each meeting that arrives is read by Claude once it's let in. What it finds waits in the Inbox as proposals. Nothing is written until you accept one.

  • Yours. Something you said you'd do becomes a Task proposal at status: inbox. You are You in the transcript, your profile name (in any common spelling, or just your first name when nobody else in the meeting has it), or an attendee at one of Your emails. We is yours too, marked low confidence so you check it. An item with two owners (You and Tobias) is made once, as yours, and the card names the other.
  • Someone else's. Something another attendee took on becomes a Task at status: waiting, with waiting_on linking their Person note — [[Tobias Fenn]]. If you have no note for them, waiting_on holds their name as text and the card says so: “There is no Person note for Lena Hart yet, so waiting_on holds the name as text.” When the name could be more than one person (two Tobiases in the meeting, or two notes for one name), a group, “Everyone” or “TBD”, nobody is guessed: the task goes to your Inbox with an empty waiting_on, and the card says who it could be.
  • Decisions become a Decision proposal, with meeting linking the meeting.
  • Follow-ups — a question left open, a “let's come back to this” — become a task in your Inbox tagged follow-up.
  • Each one cites the turn it came from. Click [[Vendor contract call#^t0004]] on the card to read what was said. Only a turn of the transcript counts: the notetaker's own next steps are given to Claude as hints, and one is kept only if a turn says it.
  • Under the title, the card says why: “Tobias Fenn took this on in Platform weekly sync, so it waits on them.”
  • Low confidence is marked. The card has a coloured edge and says low confidence when whose it is rests on a guess: the turn was spoken by Unknown or Remote Speaker, or Claude couldn't name anyone, or named someone who isn't in the meeting and has no note. A card is medium at most when someone other than you and the owner spoke the turn, and a decision or follow-up from an Unknown speaker is low. With nobody named, the card has an empty waiting_on: click Edit, set status to waiting and waiting_on to who, then Accept as edited — or accept it as yours.
  • Accept makes the task or the decision and links it from the meeting, under its Tasks or Decisions, so the meeting lists what came out of it. If your Meeting type doesn't have those two relations yet, DFAI offers to add them when the vault opens; until it has them, Accept is refused and says why — “… has no relation called tasks to link through.” The run's log and Activity say so too, once, when the Meeting type lacks them. If a note of the task's name is already there (last year's finished one, say), Accept makes yours as Send the notes 2 and links that, never the old one. A waiting-for whose Person note was renamed or deleted since is refused, saying so; Edit its waiting_on and accept again. Undo of an accept takes back both the task and the meeting's link, or neither.
  • An item that is wrong on its own is left out, and the rest are kept: one that cites a turn the meeting doesn't have, or has an owner longer than a name, or a confidence or kind that isn't one. A due date that isn't a date (or is years from the meeting) is left off and the item kept one step less sure, the log saying - adjusted …. One run makes at most 25 proposals; the log says how many it left out. The rule's log names it — - dropped "Order the cake": Platform weekly sync has no transcript turn ^t0099 that Claude was shown. — and Activity says “Left out 1 item of the answer that did not read.” A meeting with no transcript turn isn't asked about at all, and the log says so. Each part of a long meeting also starts with the last two turns of the one before, quoted for context.
  • A long meeting is read in parts, a few thousand words of whole turns at a time, so the end of an hour's call is read as carefully as the start. The log says - asked 3 times. One run asks at most 10 times, within ten minutes in all; past that (several hours of talk) the log says how much was read: - cut "…": read 193709 of 281220 characters.
  • The prompt is yours to edit. Saving the automation writes .atlas/prompts/meeting-extraction.md if it isn't there: what counts as a commitment, how sure to be, how to write a title. Change it and the next meeting is read your way. DFAI never writes over it.
  • To run it on a meeting you already have, open the automation and click Dry run to see which meetings it would read, then Run now.

What happens after a meeting

Turn on the ready-made After a meeting automation and each new meeting is read by Claude once — one run per meeting, on claude-sonnet-5-5 unless you name another model. Claude writes a short summary at the top of the meeting, and the things you plainly said you'd do become tasks by themselves, linked to the meeting both ways. You don't accept anything. What DFAI is less sure of comes as one proposal per meeting to look over, and passing remarks and other people's work don't reach you at all.

  • To turn it on: fill in your name (and, if a meeting lists you by an address your Person note doesn't have, Your emails) in Settings → Profile, then open Automations, click After a meeting and Create automation. With neither a name nor an address, DFAI can't tell which items are yours, so it makes no tasks, and the Inbox says so at the top — “DFAI can’t tell which action items are yours, so meetings make no tasks for you.” — with Open Settings, for as long as it's true. Each run's log and Activity say it too.
  • The summary at the top. Above the provider's Summary, a callout titled Summary by Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5, 2026-10-14): what the meeting was for and what came of it in a few sentences, the key points, the decisions actually made, the action items that are yours (you (a task was made), or you when they are in your review) and the ones you asked someone else for (Flag the cache before Friday: Tobias Fenn (turn t0002)) — and the questions it left open. It is short on purpose (about 150 words and six key points at most); names are spelt as your Terms spell them. The items are plain bullets, not checkboxes or links: your tasks are under the meeting's Tasks.
  • Yours to edit, never written over. The summary sits between two hidden markers. Running the rule again replaces only its own summary, and only while it is exactly as Claude wrote it: once you edit it, or delete it, DFAI leaves it as you have it and the log says so. The provider's Summary, your Notes and the transcript are never touched.
  • Which items become tasks. One you took on — you spoke the turn it cites, and Claude says it's yours — at high confidence: you said it plainly (“I'll send the contract by Friday”, or “Yes, I can do that” when asked). It lands in your Inbox (status: inbox), with due when a date was said and hasn't passed (a date that has passed is left off, and the task's body says what was said). The summary lists it as yours. DFAI decides this, not Claude.
  • One proposal to look over, per meeting. Your items Claude is less sure of (medium), yours whose turn someone else spoke, yours shared with others (Claude named you and someone else, wrote we where you spoke in the turns leading up to it, or called yours a waiting-for), something you asked another person to do and they agreed to (a waiting-for), and a decision you took part in that Claude is sure was agreed, all go into one proposal: Review what 2026-10-14 Renewal review left for you. Accept it and it becomes one task in your Inbox whose body is a checklist of those items, each linking the turn it came from; reject it and they're all gone.
  • Left out altogether. Anything Claude was unsure of (low confidence); other people's own work you didn't ask for; an item nobody, or more than one person, could be named for (a we should you said nothing around, say); and a decision you took no part in (you didn't speak in the turns leading up to it). The summary lists none of them, and the run's log and Activity count them: “… 1 proposal, left out 12 items.”
  • What isn't an item. The prompt tells Claude, with examples, to leave out work under way, ideas, maybes, questions nobody took on, plans recounted from elsewhere, how things work, and small talk. A meeting with nothing for you makes nothing. DFAI ships that prompt as .atlas/prompts/after-a-meeting.md; if you never edited it, DFAI brings it up to date when the vault opens on the Mac that runs your automations, and Activity says so. One you edited is yours and is left as it is.
  • Linked both ways. The task's source is the turn it came from ([[2026-10-14 Renewal review#^t0001]]), its meeting links the meeting, and the meeting lists it under Tasks — written in the same save as the summary, after the tasks are made, so the summary never lists a task that isn't there. It also carries atlas_item, which is how DFAI knows it made it.
  • Only meetings that arrive after the rule was last saved make tasks. A meeting that was already there — one Run now picks up from the last 14 days — is summarised but makes only the one review proposal, so turning the rule on doesn't fill your Inbox with two weeks of guesses.
  • Never twice. Running it again, Run now, or Claude reading a corrected transcript makes no second task for an item and no second summary: DFAI looks for a task with the same atlas_item in the vault and the Archive, and for a task the meeting already lists that cites the same turn — one you made by hand too. A task you ticked done and archived isn't made again. A proposal left over for an item that is now a task (from another rule, say) is refused on Accept — “… is already a task, made from the same item of the meeting; the proposal was archived and nothing else was written.” — and filed in the Archive.
  • Asked once. Claude is asked about a meeting again only when its transcript changes; a meeting whose summary is already of the words it holds isn't asked about at all. Your edits to its Summary or Notes, and the tasks DFAI links into it, aren't new words. A long meeting is read in parts, and one more ask merges the parts' summaries into one.
  • Stopped part-way? The tasks it made stay, and the log names them. If you were typing in the meeting, or it changed while Claude read it, the summary isn't written yet: the next run writes it from Claude's same answer — it doesn't ask again — and makes only the tasks still missing.
  • Summary pending. A meeting still waiting for its summary says so on its Inbox row, with why: Summary pending: Claude Code is not logged in. Fix: …, or that the rule hasn't run on it yet. Fix what it says and the rule tries again on its own. The local API and Claude's tools report each meeting's summary as written, pending, edited or none.
  • On one Mac. The rule runs where automations run: the Mac with Run automations on this Mac on (Settings → Sync). No other Mac takes over by itself; on another Mac, meetings wait until you move the automations there. Like every Claude rule it waits for a pull: if this Mac slept or stopped being the one that runs automations while Claude answered, nothing is written and it runs after the next pull.
  • Activity says what it did with each meeting: “After a meeting: Ran when a note appeared or changed (2026-10-14 Renewal review). Summary written by claude-sonnet-5-5 in 41 s for 2026-10-14 Renewal review; 2 tasks made, 2 items listed, 1 proposal, left out 4 items.”
  • Undo last run takes the summary back out, with its keys, while it is still as Claude wrote it — or, when the run wrote over an earlier summary, puts that one back — and moves the tasks it made to the Trash while you haven't changed them. One you've edited stays. If the meeting can't be written (you've edited its summary, or are typing in it), nothing of that meeting is undone and the log says why: no task is trashed while the meeting still lists it.
  • Cleaning up tasks made under the old rules. When tasks this rule made earlier aren't ones it would make now — someone else spoke the turn, or nobody named did — the Inbox offers them: “2 tasks that meetings made aren’t yours by today’s rules.” Review them lists each with why, every one ticked; untick any you want to keep, then Archive. They go to the Archive, never the Trash. A task you untick gets atlas_item_kept: true when you archive the rest or press Not now, and is never listed again. One you changed, finished or moved after the list was shown stays where it is. While DFAI can't tell who you are, it lists none and says why. How sure Claude was isn't kept on a task, so one made at medium confidence before isn't listed.

Suggested terms

Both meeting automations — After a meeting and Pull actions and decisions out of a new meeting — also notice names the notetaker seems to have misheard. Say a turn reads “Send the Bren Wick contract.” and you work with Brenwick Labs: Claude suggests the term Brenwick, misheard as Bren Wick, and it waits in the Inbox as its own Term card, never folded into a meeting's review.

  • Each one cites its turn. The card links the line it was heard in, [[2026-10-14 Renewal review#^t0001]], and says “Heard as “Bren Wick” in 2026-10-14 Renewal review.” What was heard must be in that turn exactly as written, capitals included, or the suggestion is left out.
  • Accept makes the term in Terms/ with what was heard as its Misheard as. From the next meeting on, Bren Wick arrives as Brenwick — see Misheard names, put right. Wrong spelling? Edit the card's title first, then Accept as edited. Meetings already in the vault are not changed.
  • Accept checks your vault as it is now. If, while the card waited, the spelling became a term, person or company, or what was heard became another note's Misheard as (two notes claiming one spelling would leave it uncorrected), Accept is refused and says why — ““Bren Wick” is now a misheard spelling of “Brenwick Labs”; accepted, neither would be put right.” — and the card stays. So it is when a note of the same name elsewhere would lose its [[links]] to the new term, as adding a term on the Terms page is.
  • Everyday words are never suggested as what was heard. DFAI keeps a list of the words meetings are mostly made of (the, file, tomorrow, gonna, and their plurals) and leaves out a suggestion whose misheard words are only those, whatever joins them — follow-up, step 2, mark, all count: a variant is put right wherever it stands as a word, so file as one would change every file anyone mentioned. The right spelling may be everyday words (Team Sync). What was heard needs at least three letters (a vowel sign such as the ा of राम counts as one; in Korean, two syllables are enough), so Q or PR is never suggested. Names in a script written without spaces — Chinese, Japanese, Thai — aren't suggested yet, and the log says why: DFAI can't correct a name inside unspaced text.
  • Nothing you already have. A suggestion is left out when its spelling is already a term, a person or a company, as a right spelling or a misheard one — ignoring capitals, a 's and accents on Latin letters, so brenwick, Brenwick's and Brenwíck are Brenwick. A plural is never taken for the singular: Lucas is a name of its own beside Luca, so it can still be suggested. Letters of other alphabets are never folded: Майя is not Маия. It is left out, too, when what was heard is part of a name you have (Quill, while there is a Mara Quill). A fix of accents or strokes alone is never suggested — Jose for José, Bjorn for Bjørn, Σοφια for Σοφία: the heard form is a real name too, and as a misheard spelling it would rewrite every Jose in later meetings, a different Jose included. To add a misheard spelling to a person, company or term you have, use the Terms page.
  • Suggested once. A term is known by its spelling, so a term you reject is never suggested again, and while its card waits the next meeting doesn't suggest it again. If that meeting mishears it another way (Bren Wik), the card is left as it is and the rule's log says so — ““Brenwick” is proposed already, waiting in the Inbox; once it is a term, add “Bren Wik” to its misheard spellings on the Terms page.” A different term heard the same way as a waiting one is left out too, and the log names the one waiting; so is a second term one answer suggests for the same words, and a second spelling one answer gives for the same term — the log says to add it on the Terms page once the term is made. Two automations reading one meeting at once make one card between them.
  • The log says what was left out, and why — - dropped "Sendthe": “Send the” is everyday words, and would be rewritten wherever anyone says it. — like any other item.
  • If Claude can't be asked, nothing is lost: the meeting was already corrected and linked as it was let in, and stays that way; the run's log and Activity say the step failed and why.
Your own vault's types. Matching reads email, other_emails and aliases on a person, and domains, site and aliases on a company, whether or not your types declare them. The vault in the DFAI repository declares Other emails and Email domains; add them to yours in Edit type (multi-select) to fill them in from the properties panel.

What's in a meeting file

At the top (frontmatter)

title, date, start (and end when known), kind (Notion's Category: Standup, Retro, 1:1…), provider (gemini or granola), the provider's own id, and attendees — each a name and email. A list address like platform-team@… is kept as an attendee marked group: true, and never becomes a Person.

The sections, in this order

Summary, Notes (Gemini's Details), Provider next steps, Transcript. Any can be missing; there's never a section that isn't one of these four.

  • Provider next steps are kept exactly as the notetaker suggested them, one per line: - [Tobias Fenn] Flag the cache: put the new cache behind a flag before Friday. The owner is in brackets; Granola also adds (confidence: high), medium or low at the end. They're suggestions, not tasks — nothing becomes a task by itself.
  • Transcript is one paragraph per speaker turn, with a blank line between turns. Each turn is the speaker in bold, the time, the words, and a block id of its own at the end: 
    **Mara Quill** [~00:00:00] Morning. Shall we start with the cache? ^t0001

**Tobias Fenn** [~00:09:44] Yes. It's ready, but I'd like a flag on it first. ^t0002
    The id is what lets you link to or embed one exact turn — [[2026-09-29 Platform weekly sync#^t0002]] — and quote it in another note.
  • The time. [00:09:44] is the turn's own time; [~00:09:44] with a ~ is approximate — Gemini stamps sections, not turns, so each turn gets its section's time. Gemini's times count from the start of the recording; Granola's are the time of day.
  • Who spoke. A real name as the notetaker gave it; You, which is you — read as your name from Settings → Profile (with no name there it stays You); or Unknown for anyone the notetaker couldn't name (Granola's “Remote Speaker”). An Unknown speaker is never guessed into a Person.
  • Gemini's boilerplate — the disclaimer, “Chat with meeting transcript” links, the transcript's date and end lines — is left out.

Set it up

The full steps are in tools/n8n on GitHub (the README, “API destination”). In short:

  1. Turn on the Local API. Settings (⌘,) → Connections → Local API. It says “Running on port …”; note the port.
  2. Put the token in n8n. Press Copy token. In n8n, Credentials → Add credential → Bearer Auth, named DFAI local API, with the token as Bearer Token. It lives only in n8n's credential store — never in the workflow, the vault or the DFAI repo. After Rotate token, or if DFAI comes up on another port because its own was taken, update the credential or the node.
  3. Add the HTTP Request node. In your Gemini → Notion workflow, wire an HTTP Request node from the step that assembles the meeting, beside the Notion steps. POST to http://host.docker.internal:<port>/v1/meetings, with the DFAI local API credential and a Host header of 127.0.0.1:<port>. DFAI listens only on the Mac itself and refuses any other Host, which keeps web pages out; from n8n in Docker, host.docker.internal is the Mac. Turn on Retry On Fail (3 tries, 5000 ms apart) and set On Error to continue into your failure email.
  4. Send the meeting's fields as JSON. provider (gemini), sourceId (the email's id) and title are required. The rest are optional: subject and arrived (the email's subject and when it came), attendees (each a name and email), summaryMd, transcriptMd, category, and groupAddresses for list addresses that don't look like one. Leave timeZone out and times are read on the Mac's clock; add timeZone: 'America/Los_Angeles' to pin it. Send only these: a misspelt field is refused, naming it, rather than dropped.
  5. Dry run first. On an inactive copy of the workflow, with the Notion writes deactivated (D), run once on a real email. DFAI answers 201 with the file's path and "written", and the meeting is in its month, Meetings/2026/10/. Run it again: 200, "in-vault", nothing written. A meeting with no time in its subject and no transcript comes back refused — start: the meeting has no start time — by design.

The older route, n8n committing the file to the vault's sync repository with the nodes in tools/n8n, still works while DFAI is closed; the README has it.

Check a file by hand

To see whether DFAI will accept a meeting file — one from the dry run, or one you wrote — run the same check DFAI uses, in the DFAI folder:

pnpm validate:meeting "2026-09-29 Platform weekly sync.md"

ok (with the number of turns) means DFAI will accept it. FAIL lists each problem with where it is — for example attendees[0] must be a { name, email, group } entry, not a line of text.

Meetings from Granola

Meetings you record in Granola come into the vault by themselves, as files in their month, Meetings/2026/10/, the same as Gemini's. DFAI asks Granola for them with an API key; nothing else is needed.

Set it up

  1. Make a key in Granola. In the Granola app: Settings → Connectors → API keys → Create new key, with Personal notes (and Public notes if you want meetings others share with you). It needs a Business or Enterprise plan. Granola shows the key once; copy it.
  2. Paste it into DFAI. Settings (⌘,) → Granola. Paste the key into Granola API key and press Save. DFAI keeps it in this Mac's Keychain, never in the vault, and asks Granola about it once: Settings says Key accepted, or Granola refused this key.
  3. Check Your emails in Settings → Profile. In a meeting you own, Granola calls you “me”. DFAI writes that as You when the meeting's owner is one of those addresses, and as the owner's name otherwise (so a meeting someone shared with you isn't put in your mouth).
  4. That's it. Once the key is accepted, DFAI pulls your meetings straight away. The first pull looks at every meeting Granola has and brings in the ones the vault doesn't already have.

Each Mac that should pull needs the key pasted on it. Meetings come in on whichever Mac is in front of you; the import, linking and corrections then run on the Mac that runs your automations, as for any meeting.

When it pulls

  • As the vault opens — but only after DFAI has synced the vault and caught up with what that sync brought in. That way a meeting another Mac already imported is found where it is now, and isn't written a second time. Each later sync that brings files in does the same: a pull, timed or Sync now, waits until DFAI has caught up with them — and with any sync that lands while it waits — but gives up after two minutes on any one of them. A vault that doesn't sync doesn't wait. Until then Settings says DFAI pulls from Granola once this vault has synced.
  • Every 15 minutes while the vault is open. To turn this off, add granola_periodic: false to the vault's settings note (.atlas/settings.md).
  • When you press Sync now in Settings → Granola. Sync now always looks at every meeting Granola has, so it finds one the timed pulls missed.

Settings shows Pulling from Granola… while it works, then what the last pull did and when — for example 3 new, 12 already in DFAI, 1 refused at 9:40 AM. Details lists each meeting that wasn't written and why, with Open in Granola to see it there. A pull is also a line in Activity, under Meetings — except a timed pull that found nothing new and nothing wrong, which would only be noise.

A meeting DFAI already has is never fetched or changed again — even if you edit it in Granola later. A meeting deleted in Granola stays in DFAI.

Terms

Teaching DFAI how names are spelt. A notetaker mishears the names it doesn't know — a product, a colleague, a vendor. A term is the one right spelling and the ways it gets misheard, and DFAI and Claude both use the list.

Where it is

Sidebar

Terms, at the end of the rows at the top.

Search

⌘K → Terms.

Add a term

  1. On the Terms page, press New term.
  2. Right spelling: how it should be written, for example Larkspur.
  3. Misheard as: the ways it comes out wrong, commas between them — lark spur, Larks Burr. One that holds a comma of its own goes in double quotes: "Quill, Mara", Mara Quil. You can leave it empty and fill it in later.
  4. Kind: product, person, company, acronym or other — or leave it at —.
  5. Add term. It's listed as soon as DFAI has read it.

A term is a note of the Term type, made in Terms/ and named after its spelling: Terms/Larkspur.md. A spelling a file name can't hold, like S/4 Ledger, is kept as the note's title. If the vault's Term template has a title of its own, the new term's spelling is written over it. Open a term from its name to add notes about it, or to set Refers to: it points at a Company — the company a product or an acronym belongs to — and only at companies (where the vault's Term type has it — the Terms page works without the type's file). A spelling left empty is refused: “A term needs its right spelling.”

A company's or a person's name can be added too. When [[Right spelling]] already opens a Company or Person note — say Larkspur Payroll, misheard as Larkspar Payroll — no term is made: the misheard spellings are added to that note's Misheard as (variants), after any it already lists, and the rest of the note is left exactly as it was. The page says where they went and what they now correct to: “Added to Companies/Larkspur Payroll.md: Larkspar Payroll, now corrected to “Larkspur Payroll”.” (or “… already lists those misheard spellings.”). Their name stays the right spelling, and their Also spelt still only links attendees and never corrects text. Kind isn't used for them: the note is already a person or a company. Type the name exactly as the note spells it, capitals included — corrections match exactly, so LARKSPUR PAYROLL is refused: “Companies/Larkspur Payroll.md is spelt “Larkspur Payroll”. Add the term as “Larkspur Payroll”, or rename the note first.” A note with a title of its own that isn't its file name is refused too: rename the note or change its title so the two agree, then add the term again. Give at least one misheard spelling: the name alone is refused, since the note already spells it. An archived note, or one in a hidden folder, is never added to: the vocabulary doesn't read it. A note the name opens whose properties can't be read safely (a key written twice, a bracket never closed), or a person's or company's whose variants isn't a list, is refused with the reason, and left as it is. A name that opens any other kind of note is refused as before: “[[Larkspur]] already opens Zeta/Larkspur.md, and would open the new term instead. Rename one of them first.”

Change how it's misheard

Each row's Misheard as is a box on the page — a person's or company's row too, which names the note it lives in under the name (Company · Companies/Larkspur Payroll.md) and writes that note. Change it and press Return or click away: only the variants list at the top of the note changes, nothing else in it. A variant holding a comma is shown in double quotes, so editing the others never cuts it in two; type one the same way. Two spellings that differ only in spaces are one; ones that differ in capitals are two, since DFAI matches exactly as written. A spelling with no letter or digit in it (a lone ~) is not used, and a warning says so under the term; so does one that is a word of another note's name (a lone Quill is corrected although Mara Quill is left as it is). Invisible characters pasted along with a spelling — zero-width spaces, word joiners — are dropped; the joiners that shape a Persian or Indic word or join an emoji are kept. A spelling with nothing visible in it counts as empty.

What DFAI knows

  • Every term's right spelling and the ways it's misheard.
  • Every person's and company's name, as a right spelling that is left as written, and the ways their note says it's misheard (Misheard as), which are corrected to it. The page lists the people and companies that have any, beside the terms. Their Also spelt spellings are not used to correct anything. A note is a person if any of its types is person, as @ counts it; one that is both a person and a company counts as whichever its type names first.
  • Spaces don't matter when DFAI matches, capitals do: Lark  Spur and Lark Spur are one spelling, lark spur another. A longer name is tried before a shorter one inside it, so Larkspur Payroll wins over Larkspur.
  • The top of the page counts them: “2 terms · 9 misheard spellings DFAI puts right — exactly as written, capitals included…”.
  • Templates, notes in hidden folders and archived notes don't count. To retire a term, archive it.

Conflicts

If two notes claim one spelling for two different right spellings — lark spur as a variant of both Larkspur and Larkspur Payroll — DFAI can't know which you meant, so it leaves that spelling alone. The page shows a Conflicts box above the list with each such spelling and the notes that claim it (click one to open it), and under the term: “In conflict, so not used: “lark spur””. Take the spelling off one of them and the conflict goes.

Built in

Term and Company are built-in types: Delete is turned off for both — “Term is built in — the Terms page lists its notes, and DFAI spells names by them — so it cannot be deleted.” Their properties are yours to change.

Where it's used. When a meeting arrives, its misheard names are corrected by this list — every change recorded on the meeting, and undoable from its page.

The Inbox: what Claude proposes

Claude never edits a note on its own. What it — or an automation — suggests lands as a proposal, a note waiting in Inbox/Proposals, and nothing it proposes is written until you accept it.

Open the proposals

  1. Open the Inbox: proposals are its Proposals section, above the notes waiting to be filed. The section shows while something waits for an answer, and the Inbox's count includes them.
  2. Each card says what it would make — TASK · Send Mara the payroll file — with why it was proposed and what it would write under it, how sure its maker was (a High, Medium or Low pill; a Low row has a coloured edge, so you check it first), and the line it came from: click [[2026-10-01 Standup#^t0003]] and the meeting opens at that line. A proposal that cites nothing says Cites no source, so you know to check it yourself.
  3. Answer it: Accept, Edit or Reject — each in its own colour on the row, so they are told apart at a glance.

Pick one, filter, accept the sure ones

  • Proposals that disagree are one question. Two proposals conflict when they are of the same kind, from the same line (or the same meeting), for the same slot — the owner of one meeting, say — but say different things: three links for one “Mara Quill”, or two projects for one meeting. They show as one row, Which link for owner of 2026-10-16 Platform Standup?, with a radio per answer. Accept choice accepts the one you picked and, only once that is written, rejects the rest; if the accept is refused nothing is rejected and the question stays. One Undo takes the accept back and opens the others again. Reject all rejects every one. Links to the same note count as one answer however they are written ([[Mara Quill]], [[mara quill|Mara]]), and the same answer proposed twice shows as one card: accepting it rejects the copy, and Accept all high confidence takes it once. A relation that holds many notes (a meeting's people) is never a question: each proposal for it is its own card. Different slots never conflict, and nothing about a question is saved: it is worked out from what is open each time.
  • Filters. All, High confidence (the sure ones that need no choice) and Needs a choice (the questions) narrow the list; the count beside the heading stays the whole.
  • Accept all high confidence accepts every high-confidence proposal that needs no choice, as one run, with one Undo; it shows only while there is one.

What each answer does

  • Accept writes what the card shows and files the proposal in the Archive with state: accepted and answered_via: app — api when Claude answered it for you through the Local API, so the Archive keeps who said yes. The card leaves at once and the next one can be answered straight away; what it makes is written behind it. Answered from the keyboard, the focus moves to the next card, so Enter on the next Accept is all it takes. Closing the window waits for answers still being written. The box at the bottom right says “Accepted: Send Mara the payroll file.” with Open Send Mara the payroll file to go to it, and Undo. If the write fails, the card comes back with why on it, still waiting for an answer.
  • Edit opens what it would write — the title, each property, the body (for a link, just the link) — so you can change it, then Accept as edited. A list shows as items with commas between; an item holding a comma of its own is in double quotes ("[[Larkspur Payroll, Inc.]]", [[Fenn & Co]]). A property you don't touch is written exactly as proposed. Cancel leaves it as it was. The proposal is archived with your edit, so the Archive shows what was actually accepted.
  • Reject writes nothing it proposed and files it in the Archive with state: rejected — kept, never deleted.
  • Several at once. With two or more waiting, each card has a box: tick the ones you want — Shift-click a second box to take every card between — or Select all. The bar says 3 selected with Accept 3 proposals, Reject 3 proposals and Clear. Each is answered in turn exactly as its own Accept or Reject would; one that can't be stays on the list, still ticked, with why on its card — and any card you tick while they are written stays ticked too — and the box says so — “Accepted 2 proposals. 1 was not: its card says why.” One Undo takes back every accept in the batch.
  • Undo after an accept takes away what it made — if nothing has changed it since — and puts the proposal back, open again. The box goes by itself after 5 seconds (it waits while the pointer is on it or the focus is in it), and once it has gone so has the undo; a newer answer's box takes its place. Each box's Undo takes back the accept it names: an undo still being written never takes away a later accept's Undo. A reject is said too, with no Undo. Something that failed with nothing to undo — an undo that could not be done — is said at the top of the proposals instead, and stays until you press ×.

What a proposal can make

Task, Follow-upA new task, with source linking the line it came from and meeting linking the meeting the proposal names — whatever made it from a meeting says which. A follow-up is a task to chase someone.
DecisionA new note of the built-in Decision type — title, date, meeting, project, people — citing its line the same way.
Person, Term, ProjectA new note of that type. A person proposed from a meeting is added to that meeting's People too.
Dashboard, ViewA new dashboard in .atlas/dashboards, or a saved view in .atlas/views — what asking Claude for one proposes. Only Accept in DFAI makes it, never a connected tool. Its card lists the widgets, or the view's type and layout; Edit changes only its name.
LinkAdds one link to a note's relation — Mara Quill's Companies gets [[Larkspur Payroll]]. A link from meeting linking goes in even if the meeting has changed since, but never replaces a link already there: a meeting whose project you set is left with yours.

When it says no

Accept never writes over anything. If it can't go ahead, the reason shows on the card and the proposal stays waiting:

  • “There is already a note at Send Mara the payroll file.md, which may be this one. Edit the title to make another.”
  • “Mara Quill changed since this was proposed, so it was left as it is.” — the note a link would change has been edited since; check it and answer again.
  • “2026-10-06 Payroll sync already links [[Payrun]] in project; this would replace it.” — the meeting got a project since the proposal was made.
  • A proposal, or the note it would change, with unsaved typing: save it first.
  • A proposal may only write where your notes live — apart from a dashboard or view, which goes in its own folder of .atlas — never in .atlas, a hidden folder, a folder DFAI doesn't show (node_modules), the Archive or Inbox/Proposals itself. A title too long for a file name is cut to fit.

Where they live

  • A proposal is an ordinary note of the built-in Proposal type, so it syncs to your other Mac, survives a restart, and shows in queries. Only open ones stay in Inbox/Proposals; one answered that the Archive couldn't take is listed under Answered, still in the Inbox, to archive from its page.
  • Proposal and Decision are built-in types, and every task gains a Meeting link.
  • Claude can list and answer them for you through atlas_proposals — only when you ask it to.
Proposals that arrive by themselves. An automation can ask Claude about each new meeting, or any note, and what it answers lands here as proposals. A ready-made rule for a meeting's action items is coming later in phase 29.

Tags

A tag is plain text in the note — #idea — read the same way when it is drawn, counted, clicked and renamed.

Write one

TypeIs
#ideaThe tag idea.
#tag me#A tag of several words, closed by a # later on the same line, as Bear writes it.
#area/topicA nested tag: topic under area. Nest as deep as you like.
# TitleA heading, not a tag — the space makes the difference.
tags: [idea, area/topic]Tags in the note's frontmatter count too, with or without the #.
  • A tag needs a letter in it: #1 stays a number. It is words of letters, numbers, _ or -.
  • A tag, and each part and word of it, can't start or end with _: #my_tag is fine, #_draft_ is no tag.
  • A one-letter word doesn't close a tag of several words, because its # is C# or F#: #todo fix the F# build is the tag todo.
  • The # must start the word: C#, issue#12 and a link's #heading are not tags, and neither is anything in code. Write \# to keep a # as plain text.
  • #Idea and #idea are one tag, shown as it was first spelled.

Suggestions as you type

  • After # and a letter, a list offers your tags with how often each is used. The tag you typed comes first — the one of that name, or Create #… if it is new — then longer and nested tags that match.
  • ↓ ↑ to choose another, Return or Tab to write it, Esc to close the list. For a new single word you can just keep typing.

The Tags page

  • Tags are highlighted in notes and on feed cards; click one to open it on the Tags page. Tags is also in the sidebar, under Graph.
  • The page lists every tag as a tree, nested tags under their parent, each with its count of uses. A parent's count includes its children's. Archived notes aren't counted. Sort by Name or Frequency.
  • Choose a tag to see the notes that use it, or any tag under it, with how many times; click one to open it.
  • There is nothing to clean up: a tag no note uses any more disappears from the list on its own.

Rename or merge

  • Choose a tag, then Rename. Type the New name and Preview lists the files that will change and how many uses in each. The button, now Rename n uses, changes it in every note, in the text and in tags: alike, touching nothing else in the file.
  • Tags nested under it follow: renaming #para to #area makes #para/resource into #area/resource.
  • If the new name is already a tag, the button reads Merge into #… and the two become one tag. A tag used only as the parent of others counts: renaming to #beta when #beta/y is written merges too.
  • Renaming to the same name with other capitals makes every use spelled the same way.
  • A rename reaches archived notes too, though the page doesn't count them.
  • A tag can't be moved inside itself (#para to #para/old), and a name that breaks the rules above is refused with the reason.
  • A new name of several words can't end in a one-letter word, since its closing # would read as C# does. plan a is refused: “A tag of several words can’t end in a one-letter word: #plan a# would read back as a shorter tag.”
  • Where the new name, closed with its #, would run into what follows it — #idea_ or #idea#x renamed to my tag — that note keeps the old name. The preview lists them after “1 note keeps #idea:”, each with why, such as “#my tag# can’t be written right before “_” and still read back as that tag.”
  • If another rename, in the app or over the API, has just made the new name a tag, the rename is refused at the last moment rather than merging unasked: “#… is in use already, so the rename would merge into it.” Preview again: the button will then read Merge into #….
  • A note with typing not yet saved is left alone; the message after the rename names it, so you can rename again once it is saved.
Filtering by tag. An DFAI query finds notes by tag, written in the text or in tags: alike: Tag in the builder, or tag = #idea as text, which also finds the tags nested under it. A type's own view filters only by a frontmatter tags: property, like any other property.

Automations

A rule that tidies the vault while you work: when it runs — on a clock, or when a note of a type appears or changes — which notes it takes — an DFAI query — and what it does to them: archive them, set a property, or ask Claude to propose things. Every run is logged and can be tried first as a dry run. The last run that archived or set something can be undone.

Where they are

  • Automations is in the sidebar, under Tags. It opens a page listing every rule.
  • Each rule is a note in .atlas/automations, under System in Pages. Its frontmatter is the rule; anything you write in its body is your own notes about it.

Start one

It runs every day at 03:00, takes FROM task WHERE status = done, only notes not changed in the last 30 days, and archives them.
  • After a meeting, another ready-made one, asks Claude about each meeting of the last 14 days that arrives (FROM meeting WHERE date >= @-14d) with its own prompt, .atlas/prompts/after-a-meeting.md, which DFAI writes once when you create the rule and never over yours. It is the step that says Make into tasks: My own action items, so it asks claude-sonnet-5-5 on its own budget, and your own action items become tasks without a click — see What happens after a meeting.
  • Friday review drafts your weekly review every Friday at 15:00, as one new note in Reviews/: what got done, what is stuck, what to decide. Claude is handed the weekly review itself — the tasks you finished and the meetings you had this week, who has not come back to you, what is overdue, projects with no next action, and what waits in the Inbox — and every line of the draft links the task, project or meeting it is about; a line that links none is left out, and the log says which. It writes one draft a week: Run now again that week makes nothing. Its prompt is .atlas/prompts/friday-review.md, yours to edit.
  • New automation starts a blank rule, taking every note of your first type.

When a note appears

A rule can wait for a note instead of a clock: File standups — when a meeting is created, take FROM meeting WHERE kind = standup, and archive it.

  • is created sets it off for a new note of that type, however it came: typed in DFAI, written through the Local API, saved by another app, or brought in by a sync pull — including one that arrived while DFAI was closed, once the vault is open again.
  • is changed sets it off each time a note of that type is saved with different text.
  • Neither is set off by a note moved, renamed, archived, or put back from the Archive — even if a note of the same name now stands where it was put back, and it comes back as Standup 2. A note in the Archive never sets a rule off.
  • An arriving meeting is created when the meeting import lets it in: the rule runs once, on the version stamped imported, the import's own line is never a change, and a meeting it refused or archived as a second copy never sets a rule off.
  • It acts only on the notes that set it off, and only if Which notes matches them too. The rest of the vault is left alone.
  • Once per version. The log records each version of a note the rule handled, and the version its own change left, so none of them sets it off again: not after a restart, a rebuilt index, or the same file pulled twice. Change the note again and it runs again.
  • When a note the rule has handled leaves its path — deleted, moved, renamed or archived, by you or by the rule — the log says it went: a new note made later at that path, even from the same template, is new to the rule. That holds when one pull brings both, the old one archived and a new one in its place.
  • A note whose links an archive rewrote is heard again, as anyone's edit would be — it may have been edited too. If the rule finds it as it leaves it, nothing is written.
  • A run that changed nothing isn't logged: one with nothing to do says nothing, and one that left a note alone — open with unsaved typing, say — says so in Activity only, and runs again when the note is next saved.
  • 20 runs an hour at most, per rule, counting runs that changed a note or whose query didn't read — two rules setting each other off can't run forever, and typing in a meeting doesn't use it up. Past that it is held, once: “Held back: It has run 20 times in the last hour, the most a rule may. It runs on what changed meanwhile at 10:15.”
  • Held or failing, a rule keeps what it heard — the last 20 syncs — and runs on it once its pause ends. Anything older is let go, and DFAI says so within the minute, in Activity and in the rule's log: “File standups: Let news go. Let go of the news of 3 syncs: more arrived than it holds while it cannot run. Run it by hand to take the notes they named.” Run now takes them.
  • Quit before it ran? What a rule has heard and not yet run on is kept only while DFAI is open. So each time a vault opens, a rule set off by a meeting being created looks for meetings let in since the rule was last saved that its log holds nothing of, and runs on them once, within the same caps. A meeting that was there before you saved or turned on the rule is left for Run now.
  • With sync on, it runs on the same one Mac as scheduled rules. On any other Mac nothing happens; the note waits until that Mac has pulled it. Until DFAI knows which Mac this is, what it hears is held. A rule that asks Claude also waits for a pull, as below.
  • Dry run lists the notes that would set it off now: those Which notes matches whose current version it hasn't handled. Run now takes the same notes — a way to catch up on ones it skipped while paused, or that were there before the rule.
  • In Activity its line names the notes: “File standups: Ran when a note appeared or changed (Standup). Archived 1 note.”

Ask Claude

A rule can ask Claude to read its notes and propose things. Claude never edits a note this way: it only writes proposals for you to accept, or one new note. Example: Meeting actions — when a meeting is created, ask Claude to find the action items, and propose each one as a task.

  • The prompt is a note in .atlas/prompts/, like .atlas/prompts/meeting-actions.md. Its body is what you ask; write it like a note to a colleague. In the editor you can type just its name, meeting-actions.
  • What it writes: Proposals in the Inbox — each one a task, decision, follow-up, person, term or project for you to accept, edit or reject, citing the line of the note it came from, like [[2026-10-01 Standup#^t0003]], with a confidence. Or A meeting's actions and decisions, as proposals (output: meeting-items) — see What Claude pulls out. Or the same with a summary of the meeting beside them (output: meeting-digest), which After a meeting writes at the top of the meeting. Or One new note in a folder you name, like a morning brief: never written over. On a daily or weekly schedule it writes one note each time the schedule comes round, however often it runs: run it again that day (or that week) and it makes nothing, and its log says the note is made already. A rule that runs by hand or every few hours makes a new note every run, numbered past one already there (Morning brief 2).
  • Tools Claude may use are some of the chat's read tools, like atlas_read_note and atlas_search. It's handed the notes the run is about either way. With none ticked, it reads only those.
  • It reaches Claude the way the chat does: your own Claude Code, or the API key, and the model chosen in Settings → Claude — unless the rule names its own in Model (model: claude-haiku-5-5). Leave it blank for the default, which the box shows.
  • Make into tasks (apply: mine), offered for output: meeting-items and output: meeting-digest: My own action items marks the step that writes a summary at the top of each meeting and turns your own action items into tasks, linked to the meeting, without a click; everything else stays a proposal (see What happens after a meeting). A rule saved with meeting-items is read as meeting-digest, so it summarises too. Such a step asks claude-sonnet-5-5 unless Model names another, at most 10 times a run (a long meeting's parts and the one ask that merges their summaries count together), and up to 30 times an hour on an allowance apart from the 20 runs an hour — so a backlog of meetings never uses up a rule's runs, and they never hold it back. The 30 are one allowance for the whole vault, shared by every rule that says My own action items, not 30 each, and a run never asks past what is left of them: one that starts with a single ask left asks once and says the rest of the meeting was cut. Past 30 it waits, and says until when.
  • It waits for a pull. With sync on, a Claude rule set off by a note, its schedule or opening the vault doesn't run after DFAI starts or the Mac wakes until a pull has finished and the settings still name this Mac, nor while sync is paused here. It checks again after Claude answers, before it writes anything; if the Mac slept meanwhile, nothing is written and it runs again after the next pull. A schedule that comes due while it waits runs at the next pull. Run now doesn't wait.
  • All or nothing. If Claude's answer isn't what the rule asks for, nothing is written and the run fails. That covers an answer that isn't JSON, one with a key it wasn't asked for, a proposal that cites a line the note doesn't have (or one inside a code block, or past where a long note was cut), a tool the rule didn't tick, or no answer within 10 minutes (the whole run, however many asks it makes). Activity says why: “Meeting actions: Ran by hand, and could not. Claude called atlas_search, which this rule does not let it use.”
  • Text and links only. What Claude writes can't load anything from the web when you open it: no ![](https://…) picture, no <img> or <iframe>, no web address as a property's value. An answer with one is refused whole, like any other that isn't what the rule asks for. A link you click is fine. Titles, notes and “why” are kept to a size a person would write, and properties to a few plain ones (text, numbers, true or false, short lists) — never DFAI's own, like type or archived.
  • It can't write just anywhere. Not in Chats/, .atlas, the Archive or the proposals folder, nor deeper than DFAI reads. A rule that names one is refused when it's saved.
  • Stopped part-way? If the disk refuses a write after some proposals were made, those stay, and the log says “Made 1 of 3 notes, then the write failed” with a line for each. Run now asks again and finishes the rest: a proposal already made, answered or archived is never made twice — it is known by the rule, its kind and title, and the line it cites (for a meeting, the meeting itself, wherever it has been filed or renamed), however Claude wrote the link. A run that finds them all made already says “Nothing new to propose (2 already made).”
  • Two notes with the same title? If a run reads both and Claude cites one by its title alone, the answer is refused and says to cite by path, like [[Meetings/Alder/2026-10-06 Weekly#^t0001]]; DFAI never picks one.
  • Claude Code not installed or not signed in? The run fails with the same fix the chat shows: “Claude Code is installed but not logged in. Fix: claude auth login.”
  • Activity names the model and how long it took: “Meeting actions: Ran when a note appeared or changed (Standup). Made 2 proposals. Asked claude-opus-5-5, 4.2 s.”
  • A long note is read in part. (A meeting read with output: meeting-items is instead read in parts, to its end.) Claude is shown about the first 20,000 characters of a note (less when a run reads several), and the log says so: “1 note was too long to read whole: Claude saw only the start”, with a line saying how much, like - cut "Meetings/2026-10-01 Standup.md": read 19874 of 48211 characters. A meeting is cut between turns, never in the middle of one, and a proposal can't cite a turn Claude wasn't shown.
  • On a meeting that arrives (when a meeting is created), Claude is asked once, about the meeting as DFAI let it in: checked, its misheard names put right and its people linked — never the file as it landed. A second copy of the same meeting asks nothing. A rule on meetings that change isn't set off by any of that, only by an edit afterwards. The proposals Claude makes never set its rule off.
  • 10 notes a run at most, because Claude reads each one. If more match, the log says “Read the first 10 notes, the most one Claude run takes; run it by hand for the rest”, and Run now takes the next ones. A note it was asked about counts as handled, even if it proposed nothing, on a clock or when a note sets it off alike, so the same version isn't sent again until the note changes. That holds wherever the note goes: a meeting filed or renamed out of the Inbox, its text unchanged, isn't sent again — not by Run now either. A failed run handles nothing: Run now asks again.
  • It counts toward the hourly cap of a rule a note sets off — each time Claude was asked, including an answer of nothing and a failure, so a long meeting read in four parts counts four. A run that never asked (a prompt over 20,000 characters, Claude not signed in) counts nothing.
  • It never reads a proposal, since it would only be answering itself. DFAI's own files are skipped too.
  • A rule can't say a Claude step edits a note. Anything but output: note, output: proposals, output: meeting-items or output: meeting-digest — edit, say — or a note named by path:, is refused: “… A Claude step never edits a note: it writes proposals for you to accept, or one new note.”
  • Dry run lists the notes it would ask about, without asking Claude. Its run can't be undone; answer its proposals in the Inbox instead, and keep or delete a note it made.

Dry run, run, undo

  • Dry run says exactly what the rule would do now — “Would archive 12 notes.” or “Nothing to do right now.” — then lists each note it would take (click to open) and each it would leave, with why. It changes nothing, and works before a new rule is saved.
  • Run now runs it at once, on or off, whatever its schedule. Afterwards Last run says what it did: “Archived 12 notes. Left 1 alone.”
  • Undo last run undoes the newest run that archived or set something, once. It can't reach further back: that would put back notes later runs have acted on since.
  • It reverses only what that rule did: notes it archived go back where they were, and properties it set go back to what they held.
  • What has changed since is left as it is and listed: a property changed again, a note moved out of where the run put it, or an archived note whose archive stamp isn't the run's.
  • The log is a note anyone could edit, so a line the rule couldn't have made — an archive in a rule that sets, a key it doesn't set, a value no property holds — is skipped and listed, never carried out.

When they run

  • DFAI checks the rules when a vault opens, once it is indexed, then every minute while it is open. Nothing runs while DFAI is closed.
  • A daily or hourly rule whose time passed while DFAI was closed catches up once when it opens, however many times it missed.
  • With sync on, rules run on their schedule, or when a note sets them off, on one Mac only — the one that set up sync first (a Mac connecting later never takes them), until you turn on Run automations on this Mac on another.
  • A daily or hourly rule that is on but has never run or been turned on here — written by hand, or synced in — is marked First seen in its log, and its schedule counts from then, so a daily rule runs by the next day.
  • A mark in the log dated later than now — a clock that ran ahead, or a log synced from one — is ignored, and the rule says so.
  • A rule that keeps failing waits longer each time before it is tried again: 1, 2, 4, 8… minutes, up to 6 hours. A run by hand tries it at once.
  • If its log can't be written, the rule is paused until you run it by hand, rather than change notes nothing records.
  • One run at a time; a save from the editor waits for a run under way, never dropped.

Safe by design

  • At most 500 notes are changed in one run (10 for Ask Claude). Past that it says “Stopped at 500, the most one run may do; the rest wait for the next run.” Notes already as the rule would leave them don't count.
  • A note open with unsaved typing is left as it is and logged: “It is open in DFAI with unsaved typing, so it was left as it is.”
  • A rule only acts on its own vault. Open another and the run stops: “Another vault was opened, so the automation stopped.” What it did up to then is logged.
  • An archive rule never touches notes already archived, even with INCLUDE ARCHIVED. It archives by the same rules as the app, updating links as it goes.
  • DFAI's own files in .atlas are never changed by a rule.
  • A connected Claude can read the rules and their logs and dry-run one, but never run, undo, turn on or off, make or change one — that stays on this page.

The rule's file

A rule can be written or edited by hand, anywhere. Its frontmatter:

atlas: automation
id: Archive done tasks after 30 days
name: Archive done tasks after 30 days
enabled: true
when: daily at 03:00
which: FROM task WHERE status = done
olderThanDays: 30
do: archive
KeyHolds
atlasautomation, which makes the note a rule.
idWhat its log is named after. DFAI writes it in when it first reads a rule without one, so renaming the rule keeps its history, and a new rule with a deleted one's name starts its own log. Two rules can't share one.
nameWhat the list calls it; the file's name if left out.
enabledtrue or false, nothing else — yes or 1 makes the rule broken rather than quietly off.
whendaily at HH:MM, a day of the week like fridays at 15:00, every N hours (1 to 168), on app open, manually, or a note: a meeting is created, a meeting is changed, a meeting is created or changed (an before a vowel).
whichA DFAI query: FROM task WHERE …
olderThanDaysOptional: only notes not changed in this many days, 1 to 3650.
doarchive, set with the values under set:, like set: {status: done}, or claude.
promptFor do: claude: the prompt note, .atlas/prompts/<name>.md.
toolsFor do: claude: a list of read tools, like [atlas_read_note, atlas_search]. Optional.
outputFor do: claude: proposals, or note with the folder under folder:, like folder: Briefs. Not .atlas, the Archive, Inbox/Proposals or Inbox/Meetings.
  • What set may write: plain values — text, a number, or true / false. 007 stays text, so a code keeps its zeros.
  • What it may not touch: type, id, any key starting with atlas, and the Archive's stamps archived, archivedFrom and archivedPrior: “A rule cannot set “type”: DFAI keeps that one itself.”

The log

  • One file per rule, .atlas/automations/log/<id>.md, headed <name> — run log. Each run, undo, Turned on and First seen is a ## section, newest last: when, what happened, then one line per note changed or left and why.
  • A run a note set off also lists - triggered by "…" at "…" for each note version it handled, - wrote "…" at "…" for the version its own change left, and - went "…" for a path a note it had handled left. That is the rule's memory of what it has done: delete those lines and it may act on those versions again.
  • A Claude step's run lists - proposed "…" for each proposal, - created "…" for its note, and - claude "<model>" in "…" ms, with a - cut "…" line for each note it read only the start of, - asked 3 times when a long meeting was read in parts, and a - dropped "…" line for each item it left out. What Claude wrote itself, like a reason it failed, is cut to a few hundred characters.
  • It's meant to be read. You can add your own sections, like ## Notes; DFAI keeps them.
  • It keeps the latest 100 entries, and no more than 256 KB; the oldest entries go first, never your own sections.

Activity

One place to see what DFAI did while you weren't looking, and anything that went wrong — one line per thing.

Where it is

  • Activity is in the sidebar, under Automations, with a pulse icon. A red badge counts the errors since you last opened it; opening the page clears it.

The page

  • Newest first. Each line: time, level (Info, Warning, Error), kind, and one line of what happened.
  • Show: Everything, Warnings and errors or Errors only. Kinds: Automations, Sources, Meetings, API & MCP, Claude, Index, Saves, Sync, Exports, App. Search box: Search the activity…
  • Click a line to open its note or source — or the Automations page, for a rule.

What's recorded

  • Automation runs, dry runs and undos — a run a note set off names the note; the rule's own log keeps the detail.
  • Source refreshes.
  • Meetings as they arrive: imported, archived as a second copy, or could not be imported and why.
  • Notes written through the Local API or MCP: the route and the note, never the contents.
  • Claude's edits accepted and undone, failed turns, and a chat that couldn't be saved to Chats/, in fixed wording — never your question. A line never names a chat note, since its name is the start of your question: it says “a chat note” instead. So do the notices about links left, archiving and kept edits.
  • Index rebuilds and failures.
  • Exports: what was exported, in which format, and where it went — or why it couldn't be.
  • Each sync — what it saved, brought in and sent — plus conflicts, files left out and failures.
  • Saves a note gives up on; edits kept on screen after a failed save show as a warning.
  • Writes a screen gives up on, under Saves: an edit in a view or dashboard, a view saved, a query added to a dashboard, a card added, an image, an artifact or its copy, a setting, the Local API's switch and token, a weekly review action, a task planned on the calendar or its Undo, a proposal answered, a note filed from the Inbox, Make task or its Undo, and a term added or its spellings changed. One line each. Something refused before anything is written — a name already taken, a file that isn't an image, a block changed since — isn't recorded; the screen says why.
  • Every red notice, and every warning or error said in the box at the bottom right — an Undo refused, say. One failure is one line.

Not recorded: typing, autosaves, reads, note contents, secrets, folder paths, or the “update links?” offer.

Where it's kept

On this Mac, in DFAI's data folder — one file per vault, never in the vault. About 30 days, and no more than 5 MB; the oldest lines go first. It doesn't sync.

Claude in DFAI

Ask Claude about your vault without leaving the app. It starts out knowing the page you're on, can search and read everything, and proposes changes you accept — it never writes on its own.

Ask

Ask is where a question starts: a page of its own, like Today. Press ⌘J, or Ask beside Search in the sidebar, and type. Before you ask, it lists your Recent questions (the chats kept in Chats/); pick one to carry it on. ⌘J on Ask takes you back to where you were.

  • Every answer links to the notes it came from. A link shows as a coloured chip: click it to open the note at that block, or ⌘-click for a new tab. A highlight's chip opens in the reader.
  • A paragraph, list item or quote with no link is still shown, marked not cited, and the answer's foot counts them (1 paragraph not cited).
  • Answers are text only: the only links are to your notes and to https:// addresses, which open in your browser. A link whose words don't name its site shows the site beside them.
  • Ask and the chat beside the page are one conversation, kept as a note in Chats/.

Open it

  • Press the spark button in any page's top bar (Claude beside the page (⇧⌘J)), or ⇧⌘J. It opens in the right sidebar, on its Claude tab; the same button, ⇧⌘J or the chat's own × closes the sidebar. With the sidebar already open on another tab, ⇧⌘J switches it to Claude. Not while a dialog is open.
  • What you have open comes along as a chip above the box: a note (its title, properties and text), a view (its first 20 rows), a dashboard (its widgets), or, on the Query page in DFAI query, the query you're writing and its first rows. The × on the chip (Leave this out) drops it.
  • Nothing needs turning on first: the chat doesn't use the Local API, only the way to Claude chosen in Settings → Claude.

Ask

  • Type in Ask Claude… and press Return (⇧ Return for a new line). The answer streams in; Stop ends it.
  • Under an answer, Copy copies it; under the latest one, Retry asks again.
  • Answers are drawn as on Ask: each link to a note is a coloured chip that opens the note at that block (⌘-click for a new tab), a paragraph, list item or quote with no link is marked not cited, and the answer's foot counts them (1 paragraph not cited). Long titles and words wrap to the sidebar's width.

What Claude can do

  • Search and read everything with the same read-only tools a connected Claude has over MCP: notes, types, backlinks, views, dashboards, queries, calendars, tags and the Archive. Each call shows in the conversation as a line, like Searched for “…” or Ran the query “…”.
  • Archived notes come only when you ask about them.
  • Never a whole huge folder. Every list of notes it reads — here or through an MCP client — is a page of at most 500, with the total and a cursor for the next page. Backlinks are paged too.
  • Ask about a day — “what did today's meetings say?” — and it lists that day's notes: those named for it, like Meetings/2026/10/2026-10-14 Larkspur Standup.md, and those whose date is that day. Search finds a note by the words of its title first, and by the day in its file name. If Claude asks for a note that isn't there, it's told the real notes with a name like it, so it never has to guess where one is filed.
  • It can't run automations, refresh sources or reach the web, and nothing it reads is changed.
  • It can draft for you, as proposals: the tasks of a meeting (below) and a dashboard or a view (below). Those wait in the Inbox until you answer them.

Drafting tasks from a meeting

Ask “pull the tasks out of today's Larkspur Payroll sync” and Claude runs the same reading the meeting extraction automation does, on that one meeting, now. You don't need the automation turned on.

  • The conversation shows Drafted the tasks of Meetings/2026-10-14 Larkspur Payroll sync.md as proposals. Each item — yours, a waiting-for on someone else, a decision, a follow-up — is a proposal in the Inbox, citing the turn it came from, exactly as the automation's are. Nothing else is written: no task is made and the meeting isn't touched until you accept.
  • Ask again and nothing is proposed twice — not what you already accepted or rejected, and not what the automation already proposed for that meeting. If the meeting's words haven't changed since Claude drafted it, Claude isn't asked again at all; once they change (a corrected transcript, say), it reads the meeting afresh.
  • It asks Claude as your Claude steps do, with the provider and model in Settings → Claude. A long meeting takes a while: if Claude is still reading after about 25 seconds, the conversation says it's still running, and the proposals turn up in the Inbox when it's done. If it fails then — Claude stopped, or you deleted the meeting or opened another vault meanwhile — DFAI says so in a notice and in Activity, naming the meeting, and nothing is written.
  • A note that isn't a meeting is refused: Tasks/Send it.md is not a meeting note.
  • A connected Claude can do the same with atlas_propose_tasks.

Asking for a dashboard

Ask “build me a dashboard of my tasks by status” — or a saved view, “a board of open tasks” — and Claude describes it and DFAI checks it the way the widget editor and the New view dialog check yours. A dashboard lives in .atlas/dashboards, which nothing outside the app writes, so what Claude makes is a proposal:

  1. The conversation shows Proposed the dashboard “Task board”, and the Inbox has a Dashboard card listing each widget — its kind and title — and saying Accepting makes it in .atlas/dashboards. A view's card says its type and layout.
  2. Accept writes the dashboard and it appears under Dashboards in the sidebar. A dashboard of that name already there is left alone: the new one is Task board 2. Edit lets you rename it first. Undo takes it back out.
  3. Reject writes nothing.
  • A dashboard that wouldn't draw is never proposed. If a widget counts a type your vault doesn't have, or a chart has nothing to group by, Claude is told exactly what the editor would tell you — widgets[1]: A bar chart needs a property to group by. — and fixes it before proposing. Accept checks again, in case you deleted a type since.
  • Read a query before you accept it. The card shows each widget's filters (status is inbox), and a widget that runs SQL or a DFAI query shows it whole, in monospace — what you'd be accepting is exactly what's written.

Your name (Settings → Profile)

Settings (⌘,) → Profile has two fields: Full name, the way you want it written, and Preferred name — What you go by, if it is not your first name. Press Return or leave the field to save it; Esc puts back what it held.

  • The name is kept in this vault's settings (.atlas/settings.md), not on this Mac, so every Mac that syncs the vault has it. Set it once.
  • Clearing a field removes the name; it isn't saved as a blank. Changing one leaves the other as the file holds it.
  • Invisible characters (zero-width spaces, direction marks and the like) and line breaks are dropped, and a name is cut at 100 characters, never halfway through an emoji or a flag.

  • DFAI can't read the name yet (the settings aren't read, or can't be): it asks you rather than writing [Your name], since you may well have set one.
  • It never guesses a name — not from your username, an email address, a folder path or anything else. A name set mid-chat counts from the next question.

Outside data and secrets

A source brings data from outside the vault — a calendar feed, a CSV or JSON file or web address, or someone else's SQLite database — in as ordinary notes.

A source is a note in .atlas/sources (under System in Pages) whose properties say where to read, the format (ics, csv, json or sqlite), which folder to write into, which type the notes are, and which field becomes which property. The DFAI project's own vault has one, Milestones, to copy from.

  • Open the source note and press Refresh. It shows what it Reads, where it Writes to, how often it Refreshes (only when you ask, or every few minutes while the note is open), and what the last refresh did: created, refreshed, kept your text, no longer in the feed.
  • If you've edited a note since, your text is kept; only the fields the source owns are rewritten. A record that has gone from the feed marks its note — it is never deleted.
  • The folder it writes into must already exist.
Where a source may run. A plain public feed, or a file inside the vault, works from a note anywhere. A source that sends a secret, or reads a SQLite file outside the vault, runs only when its note is in .atlas/sources (System → sources in Pages). Anywhere else, Refresh fetches nothing and says This source sends the secret “github”, so it runs only from .atlas/sources. Move the note there (System → sources) to refresh it. — or, for a database, This source reads a database outside the vault, so it runs only from .atlas/sources. Move the note there (System → sources) to refresh it. That's because other tools, like the Local API or a synced folder, can write notes in your pages but not in .atlas.

Secrets (tokens for private feeds)

Some feeds need a key. Keep it in Settings (⌘,) → Secrets, which stores it in your Mac's Keychain — never in the vault — and a source names it rather than holding it: {{secret:github}} in its address or a header, or auth: { secret: github }.

  1. Under the list, fill in the three boxes — the name (e.g. github), the value, and the sites it may be sent to (e.g. api.github.com; several separated by commas) — and press Add. All three are needed.
  2. Each secret in the list says which sources use it and Sent only to … the sites you gave.
  3. Replace… changes the value, Sites… changes where it may go, Delete removes it from the Keychain (it tells you which sources will stop refreshing).

The same list holds the key for Claude by API key: a secret named anthropic, with the site api.anthropic.com. DFAI sends it only to Anthropic's API.

Why a secret can be paused. A source note is just text — something synced in, or a tool using the Local API, could have written it — so the note doesn't get to decide where your key goes. DFAI sends a secret only over https, only to the sites you bound it to, and won't follow a redirect to anywhere else. A secret with no sites (for example one set before this rule arrived) shows Sent nowhere until you choose the sites it is for and is refused until you press Sites… and add them. Adding a site to a key that's already saved asks you in a Mac dialog first.

A secret a source names but this Mac doesn't have shows as not set on this Mac, with Set…. Secrets belong to one vault on one Mac: a vault that's moved or copied to another Mac needs them set again. DFAI never shows a secret's value back, and strikes it out of anything a server echoes.

A SQLite file

  • A source with format: sqlite, a file: and a query: turns the query's rows into notes. The query follows the same read-only rules as SQL on the Query page.
  • The file is opened strictly read-only: DFAI never writes to it or leaves files beside it. A database that's being written to (unfinished changes still pending) or that changes while it's read is refused rather than read wrong — try again later.
  • A file outside the vault must be picked with Choose file… on the source note. That choice counts only on this Mac; elsewhere, choose it again.

Share a note on Confluence

Ask Claude something like “put my Weekly sync note on Confluence, in the Team space”. Claude asks DFAI for the note as a page (atlas_export_note), then creates the page itself with its Atlassian connector, so that connector has to be connected in Claude too. DFAI never talks to Confluence and writes nothing — not to the note, not anywhere.

  • The page's title is the note's title, as its page in DFAI shows it.
  • A link becomes its words: its alias if it has one, otherwise the title of the note it names — [[mara quill]] reads Mara Quill, and a link to a heading reads Summer plans › Packing. A link to a block reads as its note's title, so a citation to a meeting line names the meeting.
  • An embed of a block or a heading (Blocks and embeds) is that block or section, quoted, under From Summer plans. One whose note or block is gone reads [Not found: …].
  • A callout becomes a quote that opens with its name in bold — Warning: Mind the gap.
  • Tasks, tables, code, headings and links and pictures on the web come across as they are.

Some things can't go on the page, and DFAI lists every one of them — Claude is told to tell you what was left out:

  • the note's properties (frontmatter);
  • where each link went — its words stay, but the page can't open a note in your vault;
  • pictures in your vault, which read [Image: …] — attach them to the page yourself;
  • files shown in place, like ![[diagram.png]], which read as their name;
  • block ids (^abc123), comments (<!-- … -->) — one you never closed hides the rest of its block, on the page as in the note — and whether a callout was folded.

A footnote in an embed is renamed after the note it came from, like [^Sources-1], so it can never be mistaken for one of this note's own.

Some blocks — one with a footnote, raw HTML or a comment in it — are tidied as written. DFAI then reads the tidied block again, and if it would come out differently on the page (HTML turned into a paragraph, say) or still points into your vault, that block goes across as plain words instead, and is listed under formatting by its first line, so you know which to fix up by hand.

Only notes outside .atlas and hidden folders are read for a title or an embed, so nothing from DFAI's own settings ever reaches the page.

Claude inside DFAI needs none of this. The chat pane (⇧⌘J) and Ask (⌘J) uses the same read tools in the app itself, with the Local API off, and proposes changes for you to accept rather than writing them.

Sync between Macs

Keep one vault the same on each of your Macs through a private GitHub repository. DFAI uses the GitHub login already on the Mac and never stores it.

Before you start (on each Mac)

  • In Terminal, run gh auth login, then gh auth setup-git. If they're missing, DFAI says so: “The GitHub command line isn’t logged in. In Terminal, run `gh auth login`, then try again.” (No gh at all: brew install gh first.)
  • The vault must not sit inside another git repository — such as the DFAI code folder. DFAI refuses: “This vault is inside another git repository (…), so DFAI won’t sync it: its notes would be mixed into that project. Move the vault out of that folder first.”

Set it up

  • Open the vault you want to sync, then Settings (⌘,) → Sync. Then one of: Your GitHub repositories — Search by name or owner, then Connect; Or create a new private repository — type a name, Set up sync with GitHub; Or paste an address — Connect existing repo.
  • Whichever you pick, the vault you have open becomes the repository, right where it is. DFAI never makes a subfolder for it, never copies it anywhere and never switches to another vault.
  • Made the repository yourself, say on github.com? Don't create another — use Connect existing repo (or Connect in the list) with its address.
  • The Mac asks first, in an DFAI sync dialog: “Connect this vault to …?” → Connect. Sync follows the repository's default branch, whatever it's called.
  • A repository that already has files is merged with the vault, not swapped for it — nothing is replaced. If both have DFAI settings and they differ, this vault's settings stay in force on the first connect. When all GitHub's version does is add settings this vault lacks, they are added to it and no copy is made; otherwise GitHub's copy is kept under Sync conflicts/atlas/ (for example Sync conflicts/atlas/settings (conflict from Studio).md) for you to take what you want from — see When both Macs changed a file below.
  • Sync counts as set up only once the repository exists and the vault has been pushed to it; then Settings → Sync says “Sync set up: this vault now syncs with its GitHub repository.” If creating it fails, the vault stays not set up — fix what the message says and try again; what DFAI already wrote (a .gitignore, a first commit) is reused.

When set-up is refused

  • The vault already sends somewhere else (an Obsidian Git vault, say) and you chose a new repository: “This vault already sends to …, so DFAI won’t make a new repository for it. To sync with that one, use Connect existing repo with its address. To use a new one instead, remove the old origin first: in Terminal, in the vault’s folder, run `git remote remove origin`.”
  • The name really is taken on your GitHub account: “You already have a repository with that name on GitHub. Pick another name, or connect that one by its address.” You only see this when it's true — any other failure says what actually went wrong.
  • A merge is half done in the vault's folder: “The vault’s repository is in the middle of a merge. Finish it, then set up sync.”

On your other Mac

  • Click the vault's name at the top of the sidebar → Open a vault from GitHub…. Paste the address, then Choose folder…; DFAI copies the vault into that folder and opens the copy.
  • This is for getting a vault you don't have open — never for syncing the one you do (that's Settings → Sync). Pick the open vault, or a folder inside it, and it's refused: “That folder is inside …, the vault you have open, so the copy would land inside it and DFAI would open the copy instead of your notes. To sync this vault with a GitHub repository, use Settings → Sync and connect it there. To open a vault from GitHub, choose a folder outside this one.” (Picking the vault itself starts “… is the vault you have open”; a shortcut or symlink to it is caught too.)
  • An empty repository is refused before anything is copied: “This repository is empty — there’s no vault in it to open. To put the vault you have open in it, use Settings → Sync and connect it there.”

While you work

  • Your changes are sent about 30 seconds after you stop — typing, an accepted Claude edit, an automation run, a move — and at least every 5 minutes while you keep working.
  • Every minute DFAI checks for other Macs' changes and brings them in; while you're typing, it waits until you stop. Unsaved typing is never overwritten; an open note reloads as after an outside edit.
  • Adjust both in Settings → Sync: When to send changes and How often to look for other Macs’ changes. Pause sync on this Mac stops it here only; Sync now syncs at once, paused or not.
  • A light at the bottom of the sidebar shows how it stands: Synced, Syncing…, N changes to bring in, N conflicts, Sync failed or Sync paused. Click it for Settings → Sync.
  • Closing the window syncs once more (waiting up to 20 seconds); anything left goes next time the vault opens.

What syncs

  • Everything in the vault: notes, pictures, .atlas (types, views, dashboards, templates, sources, automations and their logs, settings) and Chats/.
  • Not: secrets (each Mac's Keychain — set them on each Mac), the index (.atlas-cache/, rebuilt on each Mac), the Activity log, and window state.
  • Left out with a warning, and listed in Settings → Sync under Staying on this Mac, not synced:
    • a file of about 95 MB or more — “… is over GitHub’s 100 MB limit, so it isn’t synced. It stays on this Mac; to share it, make it smaller or keep it outside the vault.”
    • a folder that is a git repository of its own — “… is a git repository of its own, so only that folder isn’t synced: it stays on this Mac, and the rest of the vault still syncs. If it doesn’t belong in the vault, move it out.”
    • anything of .atlas that a line in your own .gitignore keeps out — “… is left out by a line in this vault’s .gitignore, so it isn’t synced and other Macs won’t get it. DFAI leaves your .gitignore as you wrote it; to sync it, remove that line.” DFAI never edits your lines; removing it is up to you.

When both Macs changed a file

  • Your version stays in place. The other Mac's is saved beside it as <name> (conflict from <Mac>).md; one of DFAI's own files (under .atlas) goes to Sync conflicts/atlas/ instead, so it isn't read as a second live rule or view.
  • Settings → Sync says “Both Macs changed a file. This Mac’s version was kept; the other Mac’s is saved beside it.”, and Activity gets a warning line. Nothing is lost, and git's conflict markers never land in a note.
  • DFAI's own files are merged first when they can be. For a markdown file under .atlas — a type, a view, the settings, or the record of the properties DFAI added to its built-in types — DFAI keeps one Mac's file exactly as it is and adds what the other Mac added: a new setting, or a new entry in a map such as a type's properties, copied line for line with its comments. So two Macs that each opened the vault, or ran different versions of DFAI, never leave a copy of a file DFAI wrote by itself. No copy, no warning.
  • This works when at least one of the two Macs did nothing but add: its additions go into the other Mac's file, and whatever else that Mac changed — a property removed, a label reworded — stands. A copy is still made, as above, when both Macs gave a setting different values or added the same one written differently; when neither Mac only added (each also removed, reworded, reordered, restyled or commented something, or added a blank line); when both changed the text under the settings; when the settings are a list or a single value rather than named keys, use YAML anchors (&name, *name), or have Windows (CRLF) line endings; when comments or blank lines follow the last setting, or a --- line was changed; and for the record of added properties, when you wrote anything in it — beside its list of keys or inside it, a field of your own — or took a key off it.
Automations run on one Mac. Automations — scheduled, or set off by a note — run on one Mac only, so two Macs never run the same rule. The first Mac to set up sync gets them; a second Mac connecting later — even through Connect existing repo — leaves them where they are. To move them, turn on Run automations on this Mac in Settings → Sync on the Mac you want.
No tokens. DFAI never stores a GitHub token. An address with one in it is refused: “That address has a password or token in it. Use the plain address from GitHub’s Code button: this Mac’s own GitHub login is what signs in.”

Set up a second Mac

A checklist for running DFAI on two Macs with one vault: install, open the same synced vault, set what each Mac keeps for itself, decide which Mac runs automations and the meeting import, and check it worked. It assumes the first Mac already syncs the vault.

On the first Mac, before you start

  • Settings (⌘,) → Sync says “Sync set up: this vault now syncs with its GitHub repository.”, and the light at the bottom of the sidebar says Synced. Press Sync now so the second Mac starts from everything.
  • Note whether Run automations on this Mac is on. The Mac that set up sync first has it.

1. Install DFAI on the second Mac

  1. Install the tools, once: xcode-select --install; Homebrew; brew install node@22 pnpm gh; Rust with the rustup installer (curl --proto '=https' --tlsv1.2 -sSf https://sh.rustup.rs | sh, accept the default). The README's Prerequisites has the details.
  2. Log in to GitHub, which sync uses: gh auth login, then gh auth setup-git.
  3. Get DFAI and start it. The first build takes a few minutes.
git clone https://github.com/jhoffman-dev/dfai.git ~/Projects/atlas
cd ~/Projects/atlas
pnpm install
pnpm tauri:dev

2. Open the same vault

  1. On Open a vault, press Open a vault from GitHub… — or, with a vault already open, the vault's name → Open a vault from GitHub….
  2. Paste the vault repository's address from GitHub's Code button (the plain address — one with a token in it is refused).
  3. Choose folder…: somewhere outside the DFAI folder and outside any other git repository, such as ~/Vaults. DFAI copies the vault there and opens it; the search index builds itself.
One way to sync, not two. Don't copy the vault across by hand, or keep it in iCloud Drive or Dropbox as well: two syncs writing the same files make conflicts. GitHub, through DFAI, is the one.

3. Set what each Mac keeps for itself

The vault brings its notes, types, views, templates, automations and the vault's settings (quick add types, your profile, which Google calendar). These stay on each Mac and need doing again:

4. Choose the Mac that runs automations and the meeting import

  • One Mac does both. Automations and the meeting import run only on the Mac with Run automations on this Mac on, so two Macs never run the same rule. Connecting the second Mac leaves them where they are.
  • Pick the Mac that is awake most of the day. n8n sends each meeting to the Mac it runs on, and that Mac's DFAI, open with the Local API on, writes it into the vault. The importing Mac files it in the Inbox at its next pull after the file reaches it — within a minute or so if it's the same Mac, otherwise once sync brings it over.
  • It waits for a pull after starting or waking. Even the chosen Mac imports nothing, and runs no Claude rule, until a pull has finished since DFAI started or the Mac last woke, and the settings that pull brought still name it. After a night asleep it catches up as soon as that first pull is done. While Pause sync on this Mac is on, it waits; Settings → Sync says so.
  • To move them, turn on Run automations on this Mac in Settings → Sync on the Mac you want. Until the other Mac next syncs and reads that, both may import a meeting; they come to the same answer for every file, and sync keeps both if they write the same one. A Mac that was asleep when you moved them reads it at its first pull after waking, before it imports anything.

5. Check it on the second Mac

  1. The light says Synced, and the sidebar shows the same notes, types and views as the first Mac.
  2. Press ⇧⌘D: today's note opens — the same one the first Mac made today, once it has synced; made here otherwise.
  3. From another app, press ⌃⌥N, type a line and press Return: it's in the Inbox here, and on the first Mac within a couple of minutes.
  4. Edit a note on the first Mac; it appears here within about a minute, or at once with Sync now.
  5. Settings → Sync on each Mac: Run automations on this Mac is on on exactly one of them.
  6. The light never says Sync failed or N conflicts; if it does, click it — Settings → Sync says what happened.

Your files

  • Every note, type, view and dashboard is a markdown file in your vault. DFAI writes back only what you changed; everything else keeps its exact bytes, so Obsidian and git see clean edits.
  • Links are stored as [[Name]], the same way Obsidian writes them. A bookmark is the same link alone on its line followed by <!-- atlas:bookmark -->, a comment Obsidian's reading view and GitHub hide; nothing else about the card is stored.
  • A block link or embed is [[Note#^id]] or ![[Note#^id]], with the ^id written after its block, as Obsidian writes it — see Blocks and embeds. A - [ ] line with nothing after it is an empty task.
  • Tags are stored as you typed them, #tag in the text or a tags: list in the frontmatter. Obsidian reads single-word and nested tags the same way; it reads #tag me# as #tag followed by text.
  • Archived notes are ordinary files in Archive/, at the path they had, with archived and archivedFrom in their frontmatter, so Obsidian still opens them.
  • Frontmatter written flow-style, {status: done, tags: [a]}, and files that start with a byte-order mark are kept intact when DFAI changes a property.
  • .atlas/ holds types, templates, views, dashboards, sources, automations and settings. It shows as System in Pages — all but the templates, which are on the Templates page, and the views and dashboards, which have their sections.
  • A template is a plain markdown file in .atlas/templates/, named for what it makes: Person.md, Daily.md, Task.md, Artifact.md.
  • Each automation is a note in .atlas/automations/, and its run log a note in .atlas/automations/log/, named after the rule's id. Both are plain markdown you can read, diff and edit.
  • The Activity log is not in the vault: one file per vault in DFAI's data folder on this Mac, about 30 days or 5 MB.
  • A link (symlink) in your vault that points into .atlas or .git doesn't open — DFAI refuses it, so nothing can reach DFAI's own files by a side door.
  • The search index lives in .atlas-cache/. It's only a copy; if it's ever wrong, Settings → Index → Rebuild.
  • Pictures you add go in attachments/ (or beside the note, if you chose that in Settings → Images). Thumbnails DFAI makes of pages live in .atlas-cache/thumbnails; an artifact's is kept with its saved copy.
  • Each chat with Claude is a note in Chats/, written once and then only added to. Claude's proposed changes are written only when you press Accept, never into .atlas, a hidden folder or Chats/.
  • Proposals wait as notes in Inbox/Proposals/; answered ones move to Archive/Inbox/Proposals/ with their state.
  • A synced vault is a git repository; DFAI keeps its own lines in .gitignore (the index, Finder and Obsidian per-Mac files). See Sync between Macs.
  • Secrets live in the macOS Keychain, never in the vault. The chat's settings (which way to reach Claude, the model) are kept on this Mac; your name is in the vault's settings, so it syncs.
  • Nothing leaves your Mac unless you turn on the Local API, connect a source, turn on sync (the vault goes to your GitHub repository), or ask Claude in the chat — then what you ask, the page on the chip and what Claude reads go to Anthropic, through Claude Code or your API key.
